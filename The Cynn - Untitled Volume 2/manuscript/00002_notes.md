### Spotted King’s Court: First Encounter / Wodbora Notes

The first encounter establishes that the company is badly outmatched by the supernatural forces around the Spotted King.

* **Elswyth / Monanleoht:** Monanleoht barely keeps Elswyth alive because Fussleton can contend with one redcap possessing comparable powers. Against multiple redcaps, however, he is outmatched.
* **Osric / Barrow’s Edge:** Osric enters Barrow’s Edge and the shadow fyrd protect him from being killed, but they cannot defeat the redcaps either.
* **The other humans:** Ridiculously overmatched.
* **Cynewulf:** Forced to consider drawing Wodbora.

### Wodbora’s Contract

The key rule is that the **confessed sin must match the weight and moral shape of the intended draw**. It is not a simple scale where worse sins purchase more supernatural power.

Cynewulf realizes he has a precise match.

His confession is the week when, drunk and armed, he terrorized a helpless family for amusement. He repeatedly threatened them with violence without actually carrying it out.

His proposed draw does exactly the same thing:

* He intends to summon enough of the Wodhere to convince the redcaps that catastrophic violence is imminent.
* He does **not** intend to release the full storm.
* In doing so, he will also knowingly terrify his own companions, who understand what Wodbora can unleash.
* Therefore, he is once again making helpless people live under the immediate possibility that he might unleash terrible violence.

The equivalence is exact: **terror through withheld violence**.

### The Penance

Cynewulf proposes the penance to the ravens **before drawing**.

Afterward, he must:

* Tell the entire company about the family he terrorized.
* Explain why that sin matched what he just did to the redcaps and to them.
* Admit that he knowingly used his companions’ fear as part of the draw.
* Surrender his position as leader to their judgment.
* If they decide he is no longer fit to lead, he will accept their decision and follow instead.

The penance therefore restores agency to the people he frightened. The original family had no choice about remaining subject to his power. His present companions will.

### The Second Witan

This encounter deliberately reverses Cynewulf’s first experience with the ravens.

The first time, **Cynewulf was uncertain and the ravens guided him through the contract**.

This time, **Cynewulf is confident and prepared**, while Huginn and Muninn slow the witan down with objections. He knows the original bargain and insists they honor its precise terms.

Muninn reminds him:

> “You said you’d never draw the blade again.”

And later challenges him:

> “You promised to protect this blade. Now you wish to draw it before all of your companions. Perhaps we should deny you in principle.”

Cynewulf answers:

> “That is NOT the contract. All you can deny is my penance.”

This establishes that the ravens may object morally, remind him of previous promises, and judge whether his proposed penance is adequate, but they **cannot invent a new veto over the draw itself**. Their authority is limited by the contract Huginn originally explained.

Cynewulf effectively forces the witan to distinguish between:

* whether drawing Wodbora is wise,
* whether Cynewulf is breaking his personal promises,
* and whether the proposed sin, draw, and penance satisfy the actual magical agreement.

### Cynewulf’s Final Safeguard

When challenged over who determines whether he has drawn far enough, Cynewulf offers himself as security:

> “Let the Wodhere bind me if I judge incorrectly. But I will have sheathed the blade before it leaves me.”

His logic:

* Cynewulf himself judges when the terror of the present draw equals the terror of the confessed sin.
* He will sheathe Wodbora as soon as that point is reached.
* If he judges incorrectly, the Wodhere may punish or bind **him**.
* But the blade will already be closed, preventing his mistake from escalating into the full storm and endangering everyone else.

### The Actual Draw

Once the ravens accept the penance:

* Cynewulf draws Wodbora only far enough for the Wodhere to begin manifesting.
* The redcaps recognize what is coming.
* His companions are terrified as well.
* The threat itself accomplishes what Cynewulf intended.
* Before the Wodhere fully arrives, he closes Wodbora.

**Click.**

The redcaps have been terrorized into retreat without the Wodhere actually being unleashed.

Cynewulf then has to wait and discover whether his judgment of the balance was correct... and afterward fulfill the penance he promised.

The scene demonstrates growth through **precision and restraint**, not harmlessness. Cynewulf remains terrifyingly capable. The difference is that he now understands the danger well enough to define the limits beforehand, bind himself to consequences, and stop exactly where he said he would.

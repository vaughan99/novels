Osric rewrites the Origin of Ketill's story to be now.

Osric is not merely **retelling** Ketill’s Finn story onto the demon. He is effectively **rewriting the story’s origin so that this present encounter becomes the event the old story was always describing**.

That is much more powerful, and it fits the title **Origin-Bringer** literally.

The original relation is:

> Long ago, a Finn encountered supernatural water.
> Travelers had to give their names to pass.

Osric’s act changes the metaphysical genealogy:

> No — this is where that story begins.
> This demon is the Water.
> We are the travelers.
> What Ketill heard generations later is an echo of **this**.

He doesn’t change the words of the later tale so much as **replace its source event**.

That creates a really elegant distinction between ordinary storytelling and Origin-Bringing:

**Storyteller:** tells a story about something that happened.
**Origin-Bringer:** causes something happening now to become what the story was about.

And that answers why Osric can’t simply invent convenient nonsense:

> “Once there was a demon who immediately died.”

There is no downstream story for reality to attach to. No established narrative has come back upstream looking for an origin.

Ketill’s Finn story does.

Osric recognizes a story already circulating in the world and says, in effect:

> **This is what really happened.**

Then causality bends backward enough to accommodate him.

That is wonderfully dangerous, too, because the resulting story may no longer remain exactly the way Ketill remembers it. Osric has just created its **true source**, and everything they do now potentially determines details that storytellers will later garble, preserve, embellish, or misunderstand.

So if One-Eye complains:

> “Ketill never said there were six travelers.”
>
> “Maybe Finn didn't remember six.”
>
> “There wasn't a Finn!”
>
> Ketill shrugged. “There is now.”

You get a beautiful temporal/narrative loop without needing literal time travel.

And it connects directly to dying wyrd. Wyrd normally makes stories seem inevitable **afterward**. Osric can do something stranger: create an origin that makes an already-existing story seem inevitable **beforeward**.

That makes Origin-Bringing almost an emergent replacement for wyrd:

**wyrd makes endings converge; Origin-Bringers make beginnings propagate backward through story.**

That could become extremely important later.

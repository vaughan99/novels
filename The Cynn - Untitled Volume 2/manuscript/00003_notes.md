Here’s a clean notes version of the **current stable of candidate Cynn narrative-warfare devices**. This is a pool to draw from, not a final set.

## Personal / vocation-linked gifts

**One-Eye: illusion-dispelling arrows**
A quiver of arrows that dispel illusions on impact. Likely paired with a perfect mundane bow and hunting kit. One-Eye may initially be offended that the Cynn made him a replacement bow, as though his old one were inadequate.

**Tor: Runes of Glamour**
A fist-sized sphere of ball-bearing-type steel. The word **Change** appears on it in ever-present runes. Saying “Change” transforms it into any object of roughly equal size, but the runes must remain outwardly and plainly readable in some form. Saying “Change” again restores the steel sphere.

The joke is especially good for Tor because he is the skeptic. His glamour cannot hide the fact that it is a glamour.

**Ketill: Box of Assembly**
A chest with the imprint of Ketill’s hands in velvet on the bottom. Place properly pre-crafted components inside and wish for the completed item. The box instantly assembles them.

It does not replace craft knowledge. Scales, lamellae, cordage, leather, wood, fittings, etc. must already be correctly prepared. Ketill still does the skilled work. The box eliminates assembly labor.

**Murdock: Targe of Grains**
When activated, the targe whirls sparkling grains of sand into the air. This dazzles many fey creatures and is particularly effective against obsessive beings compelled to count every grain. It is a supercharged version of traditional salt/sand/grain fey wards.

## Company spirit-road equipment

Not personal gifts. These are infrastructure for the whole company.

**Spirit-Sight Blinders**
Blinders fitted to animals so they can perceive the spirit road.

**Spirit-Binding Horseshoes**
Horseshoes that bind the horses to the spirit road once they enter it.

**Spirit-Bound Wagon Wheels**
Replacement wagon wheels that similarly keep the physical wagon attached to spirit geography.

The transport logic is:

**see the road → remain on the road → carry the company along it**

The wagon does not magically locate the road.

## General Cynn / Besidius narrative-warfare devices

### Book of Endless Endings

Each page contains a possible ending to a story currently being imposed by an enemy.

Rip out a page and throw it, and the enemy must adhere to that ending.

The ending must fit the narrative already established. It is not arbitrary reality alteration. The more completely an enemy commits to its story, especially through monologuing, the more vulnerable it becomes.

Ideal anti-monologue weapon.

Possible absurd ending:

> “Quick... give him the everyone-joins-a-dance-off ending!”

Core principle:

**If you insist this is a story, then the story gets an ending.**

### Dust of Who Am I?

Identity-disruption dust.

Thrown onto a creature, it causes uncertainty about its own identity, role, title, nature, allegiance, curse, or narrative function.

Especially useful where supernatural power depends on fixed identity.

It does not necessarily change what the creature is. It makes the creature uncertain what it is.

Potentially disastrous around One-Eye.

### Mirror of Watch-Out!

Show the mirror to an enemy.

The enemy sees its **worst enemy standing behind it**.

That worst enemy may be a person, creature, memory, force, symbol, weakness, fate, or even itself.

The mirror causes panic, distraction, hesitation, or accidental revelation. It need not compel a specific behavior.

Examples:

* witch sees the child she failed to destroy
* wolf sees the woodcutter
* false king sees the rightful heir
* creature vulnerable to dawn sees sunrise
* enemy whose worst enemy is itself sees itself

### Skull of Mocking

A floating skull that attaches itself to a target and mimics **every word the target says**.

It cannot invent dialogue. It only repeats.

Useful for:

* destroying concentration
* ruining solemn pronouncements
* mocking curses
* disrupting ritual speech
* humiliating supernatural authorities

Particularly nasty when a spell is reversed or cancelled by repeating the same words back to the caster.

Its entire operating principle remains gloriously stupid:

**Target speaks. Skull repeats target.**

### Beans of Stalking

A play on *Jack and the Beanstalk*.

Plant or throw a bean and it grows into a humanoid stalker made from vine and leaves:

* stalk-body
* vine arms
* legs/root-feet
* leafy ears
* a head capable of peeking around things

It follows the designated target:

* peers around hedgerows
* climbs trellises
* crawls commando-style
* hides behind walls
* freezes when noticed
* resumes pursuit when the target looks away

Crucially:

**It never catches the target.**

Its purpose is not pursuit-to-capture. Its purpose is to make the target increasingly certain that something is following them.

> “What will it do if it finally catches them?”
>
> “It never catches them.”
>
> “That’s not very useful.”
>
> “It is if you want to make them think something’s after them.”

A flawless **stalking** mechanism, not a pursuit mechanism.

### Deck of One More Things

The Columbo artifact.

A reusable deck that provides concepts that can justify:

> “Oh... one more thing.”

It is especially useful when someone with authority tries to prematurely end a conversation:

* guards
* gatekeepers
* officials
* ferrymen
* supernatural threshold-keepers
* petty bureaucrats

The card drawn supplies a concept the “one more thing” must genuinely relate to.

Could use tarot, but need not. A bespoke deck of story-elements may fit better:

* Guest
* Blood
* Road
* Debt
* Name
* Mother
* Child
* Door
* King
* Promise
* Grave
* Fire
  etc.

Reusable, and there is no reason not to draw a hand of several cards beforehand.

It does **not** compel agreement. It prevents premature dismissal long enough for one additional relevant issue to be heard.

The Deck attacks a person who refuses to allow **enough story**, while the Book of Endless Endings punishes someone who tells **too much story**.

### Horn of Narrative Elasticity

A reversible horn with opposite effects depending on direction.

**Speak through it:**
Your words become absurdly over-narrated, florid, ceremonious, and extended.

**Listen through it:**
The horn strips away euphemism, ceremony, intimidation, legal clutter, and bullshit so you hear the actual bargain or proposition.

Example:

> “You may dwell beneath my roof for three nights and partake freely of my table under the ancient protections...”

Through the listening end:

> “Eat here and you owe me.”

It does not necessarily detect lies. It reveals what the proposed bargain **means**.

Alternative informal name:

**Horn of the Long Story Short**

### Chair of Boing

A deliberately ridiculous reality-kinetics chair.

Its precise final mechanics are not settled, but its role is to weaponize bouncing / kinetic absurdity while remaining recognizably a chair.

Battle cry:

> “Bring forth the Chair of Boing. Enemies beware! The Chair shall provide no mercy!”

Strongly reminiscent in tone of the Comfy Chair: absurd object introduced with terrifying ceremonial seriousness.

Unlike the Comfy Chair, this one may actually be tactically dangerous.

---

### Asking Mask of Tasks

A mask that weaponizes polite requests.

You must **ask** someone to do something. If the target voluntarily assents to the request, even casually, the accepted request becomes a task they are compelled to complete.

Commands do nothing.

> “Drop your sword!”
>
> Nothing.
>
> “Would you mind dropping your sword?”
>
> “Fine.”
>
> *clatter*

It is powerful against beings bound by hospitality, manners, ritual courtesy, pride, or compulsive obligation.

The key limitation is consent to the request.

---

### Tasking Mask of Asks

Not to be confused with the Asking Mask of Tasks.

This is the **Jeopardy! magic item**.

You supply an answer.

The target is compelled to formulate the question that makes that answer correct.

Example:

> “Count Dracula.”
>
> “Mmm... no.”
>
> “Tell me. Count Dracula.”
>
> “Who is my master and is controlling me right now? DAMMIT!”

This makes it an excellent reverse-interrogation device.

It can potentially reveal:

* allegiances
* locations
* causes
* identities
* passwords
* motives
* ownership
* weaknesses

But the interrogator must first supply a true or relevant answer.

### The Two Masks as a Matching Pair

The **Asking Mask of Tasks** and **Tasking Mask of Asks** should be supplied together as an intentionally matched set.

When turned against each other, they risk creating recursive narrative feedback:

* one generates requests
* the other generates questions
* those questions can create new tasks
* those tasks can produce new answers

Potential result: grammatical mutually assured destruction.

Besidius should probably warn against pointing them at each other.

Probably after having done it once.

Absolutely. Here’s a clean notes version of the **new narrative-warfare items added today**.

### Plot-Thickening Potion

A potion that literally **thickens the plot**.

Pour it into something currently carrying the scene, such as a witch’s cauldron, and the narrative suddenly acquires new complications:

* estranged relatives
* disputed inheritances
* old promises
* unexpected visitors
* hidden grievances
* mistaken identities
* unrelated but suddenly relevant subplots

It does not help the party directly. It simply makes the current story more complicated and therefore less inevitable.

Example:

* Pour it into a witch’s cauldron.
* The stew visibly thickens.
* Someone knocks at the door.
* Suddenly the witch’s estranged aunt has arrived.

Core principle:
**If the enemy has simplified the story into one inevitable outcome, make the story messier.**

---

### Cloak of Dark and Stormy Nights

A mood-setting cloak designed for **atmospheric warfare**.

When used, it imposes the feel of a dark and stormy night on the surrounding scene:

* gathering clouds
* ominous wind
* thunder
* lightning
* rattling shutters
* creaking trees
* flickering lights
* general gothic foreboding

Useful against overly cheerful fey such as:

* sprites
* pixies
* redcaps pretending to be harmless woodland gnomes

It attacks the **mood supporting their deception** rather than the deception itself.

Key exchange:

> “What does it protect you from?”
>
> “A cheerful disposition.”

---

### Fairy Salt

Formerly considered fairy dust. Better as **Fairy Salt**.

Stored in Fussleton’s old child-skull salt container and measured with the toe-bone spoon.

A pinch adds **fairy flavor** to whatever it touches.

Possible uses:

* mixed with oats to help horses function on the spirit road
* sprinkled on loads to make them feel lighter
* countering supernatural melancholy or oppressive gloom
* adding a faintly fey quality to food, drink, objects, or environments
* generally making something more compatible with fairy-story logic

Important distinction:
It does not force happiness. It removes or softens supernatural emotional burden.

The container itself is part of the joke:
**child skull + toe-bone spoon + cheerful fairy salt**

---

### Belt of Carrying the Story

Originally considered as a bag, but a **belt** works better.

When willingly buckled on, the wearer takes on a narrative burden that would otherwise be shared by the group.

Possible burdens:

* curses
* supernatural grief
* shared pursuit
* magical obligation
* prophecy
* doom
* narrative debt
* hostile story roles

The burden is **not reduced**.

It is concentrated.

Core question answered by the item:
**Who carries this?**

If the belt is removed, the burden redistributes.

This is one of the more serious Cynn items.

---

### Fire Bundle of Sunrise

A tied bundle of herbs, tinder, shavings, or similar material that creates **portable sunrise** when lit.

It does not merely make light.

It creates the **narrative condition of dawn** in the immediate area.

Useful against:

* vampires
* stone trolls
* night-bound curses
* creatures vulnerable to sunrise
* bargains lasting “until morning”
* enchantments tied to darkness
* anything dependent on a “before dawn” clause

It may create:

* pale dawn light
* birds beginning to call
* warming air
* shortening shadows
* perhaps an impossible rooster crow

Core principle:
**It attacks the word “until.”**

Besidius logic:

> “It doesn’t kill vampires.”
>
> “You just said it does.”
>
> “No. Sunrise kills vampires.”

---

### Candle of Shadow-Casting

A candle that causes anything passing between it and a wall or surface to cast the **mythic, enlarged, archetypal version of its shadow**.

Examples:

* cat → lion
* dog → giant hound
* horse → monstrous warhorse
* man → giant or legendary warrior
* crow → enormous raven
* rabbit → ominous giant hare

The physical subject remains unchanged.

Only the shadow tells the bigger story.

It becomes especially powerful when combined with **Tor’s Runes of Glamour**:

* transform the orb into a tiny dragon
* place it near the candle
* project a gigantic dragon shadow onto a wall

This combination can create almost any menacing giant silhouette.

It also leads naturally to Tor accidentally inventing the first **drive-in shadow theater**, using the wagon, candle, wall, and transformed figures as moving projections.

---

### Spear of Character Skewering

A spear with an **ethereal blade** that does not physically wound the target.

Instead, it skewers and removes the traits that make the target narratively distinct.

It strips away:

* personality
* charisma
* mannerisms
* dramatic identity
* pride
* cunning
* theatricality
* unique behavioral traits
* whatever makes that character memorable

The victim remains alive and may retain memory, skills, and rank, but becomes utterly **boring and indistinguishable**.

Example:

> “I am the mighty Frog King, Lord Blaphemorikrinklebein! Behold my vast army of...”
>
> *spear strikes*
>
> “Ribbit.”

The Frog King is still technically king.

He is simply no longer much of a **character**.

#### One-use attack rule

The spear can only skewer **one character**.

Afterward, it permanently embodies the traits it stole.

So after skewering the Frog King:

* the victim becomes narratively beige
* the spear becomes pompous, regal, boastful, charismatic, frog-kingly, etc.

The spear is not destroyed.

It is simply **full**.

It can never strip another character again.

This makes its use a major decision while leaving behind a strange new “character artifact.”

---

### Powdered Wig of Wigging Out

Rejected for Book 2.

The concept was:

* powdered wig
* wearer progressively “wigs out”

Funny, but too anachronistic for the 10th-century setting.

Keep in reserve for a later-period Cynn story.

---


### The Book of Forgotten Punchlines

So the Book of Forgotten Punchlines contains complete jokes, riddles, sayings, little comic stories, etc., except the final line is missing.

The reader reads the page aloud to the target.

The magic ensures that the target recognizes it as something they once knew by heart.

Every word.

Every beat.

Every setup.

Except...

the punchline.

And that is the torment.

## Useful thematic note

These new items continue the core Cynn principle:

**Do not attack the monster directly. Attack the narrative condition that makes the monster dangerous.**

Examples:

* simplify the enemy too much? **Plot-Thickening Potion**
* cheerful fairy atmosphere hiding danger? **Cloak of Dark and Stormy Nights**
* supernatural gloom? **Fairy Salt**
* group-wide curse? **Belt of Carrying the Story**
* protected by night? **Fire Bundle of Sunrise**
* intimidation needed? **Candle of Shadow-Casting**
* villain too narratively powerful because of personality? **Spear of Character Skewering**

The arsenal is becoming less like magical equipment and more like a **field kit for sabotaging story logic**.


## Larger design principle

These devices work because they are **narrative warfare**, not generic enchanted equipment.

They attack things such as:

* identity
* endings
* bargains
* rhetoric
* fear
* compulsions
* authority
* hospitality
* attention
* transformation
* storytelling itself

The joke is often also the mechanic.

The Cynn do not solve supernatural problems by making a sword that hits harder.

They ask:

**What stupid rule is this nightmare using... and what equally stupid object would make that rule betray it?**

That is the stable.

# Cynn Healing and Healing-Adjacent Items

The Cynn approach to healing follows the larger logic of **narrative warfare**. Their devices do not simply restore hit points or erase wounds. Instead, they interfere with different parts of the story surrounding injury: how severe it was, when recovery occurs, how others perceive it, whether the injury is allowed to reach its conclusion, or how the injured person experiences what happened.

## Wrap of Not As Bad As You Thought

**User concept.**

A magical wrap applied to an injury. Rather than directly healing the wound, it revises its severity downward to the least catastrophic version that remains reasonably compatible with what happened.

Examples:

* The artery was missed.
* The bone was cracked rather than shattered.
* The burn was shallower than everyone feared.
* The spear somehow passed between the important organs.
* The bleeding looked far worse than it actually was.

It cannot turn a severed limb into a bruise or undo something unquestionably catastrophic. It finds a plausible version of events in which the injury was **not as bad as everyone thought**.

The victim still requires ordinary care afterward. The Wrap primarily turns certain death or permanent catastrophe into a survivable medical problem.

Possible Besidius explanation:

> “It doesn’t make you better.”
>
> “Then what does it do?”
>
> “Makes you less worse.”

**Narrative axis attacked:** severity.

---

## Potion of Here Take This And You'll Feel Better By Morning

**User concept, subsequently refined through discussion.**

The potion does exactly what the name promises. Someone drinks it and, **by morning**, their condition has substantially improved.

It does not necessarily restore them completely. Rather, the body reaches the stage of recovery that ordinarily comes after surviving the dangerous night:

* fever breaks,
* swelling subsides,
* bleeding stabilizes,
* pain becomes tolerable,
* exhaustion lessens,
* the patient is clearly recovering rather than deteriorating.

Its great loophole appears when combined with another Cynn device.

### Interaction with the Bundle of Sunrise

The **Bundle of Sunrise** creates the narrative condition of sunrise wherever it is lit.

Therefore:

1. Drink the Potion of Here Take This And You'll Feel Better By Morning.
2. Light the Bundle of Sunrise.
3. It is now morning.
4. The potion's condition has been satisfied immediately.

The two items together create an accidental form of **instant healing**, even though neither item individually provides instant healing.

Possible exchange:

> “You can do that?”
>
> “Apparently!”
>
> “You made them.”
>
> “Yes, but not *together*.”

This is a particularly good example of Cynn artifacts combining through literal narrative logic.

**Narrative axis attacked:** time.

---

## Censer of I Feel Fine

**User concept, with an important user correction to the original interpretation.**

For one hour, the Censer convinces everyone around the injured person that **the injured person feels fine**.

It does not relieve pain.

It does not heal anything.

It does not even convince the injured person that they are healthy.

Instead, the smoke goes up **everyone else's rear end**, because everyone else is the one who must be persuaded.

Thus someone may be visibly bleeding, limping, shaking, or carrying an arrow through them, while everyone around them nevertheless thinks:

> “He seems fine.”

Example:

> “You need to sit down.”
>
> “I’m fine.”
>
> “...you know, you do seem fine.”
>
> “I have an arrow through me.”
>
> “Yes, but you’re handling it remarkably well.”

When the effect ends, everyone suddenly recognizes the condition that was there all along:

> “WHY ARE YOU BLEEDING?”
>
> “I HAVE BEEN BLEEDING THE ENTIRE TIME.”

This is therefore not really a healing item. It is **medical gaslighting in a thurible**.

Potential uses include concealing weakness from enemies, preventing companions from interfering with an injured person's plans, or allowing someone to pass inspection while obviously unwell.

**Narrative axis attacked:** perception.

# Additional Candidate Healing Items

## Stitch of Not Yet

**Assistant suggestion.**

A magical thread or stitch that prevents an injury, curse, poison, or mortal condition from reaching its conclusion.

It does not cure the problem.

It simply tells the story:

**Not yet.**

A dying person can remain alive. A poison can remain short of its fatal stage. A wound can remain short of irreversible collapse.

The underlying condition still exists and must eventually be treated. The Stitch merely postpones the ending.

This fits particularly well with the larger Cynn philosophy because it attacks one of the most fundamental narrative structures:

**the conclusion.**

**Narrative axis attacked:** finality.

---

## Cup of You'll Laugh About This Later

**Assistant suggestion.**

The Cup does not heal physical injury. Instead, it prematurely gives the injured person the emotional perspective normally acquired much later, after surviving something terrible.

The person experiences the event partly as something already survived.

Fear, shock, humiliation, or trauma loses some of its immediate power because the victim receives the emotional distance implied by:

**“You'll laugh about this later.”**

The physical injury remains unchanged.

This could be especially useful for someone who must continue functioning after a terrifying experience but is physically capable of doing so.

**Narrative axis attacked:** trauma / emotional consequence.

---

## Bandage of That Looked Worse Than It Was

**Assistant suggestion.**

A more appearance-focused cousin of the Wrap of Not As Bad As You Thought.

Rather than substantially changing the actual injury, it reduces the apparent horror of it:

* less visible blood,
* less grotesque swelling,
* bruising appears less severe,
* exposed damage becomes less alarming.

Its main function would be suppressing panic, preserving morale, or concealing vulnerability.

This overlaps considerably with both the Wrap and the Censer, so it may be redundant.

**Narrative axis attacked:** spectacle.

---

## Splint of Stay With Me

**Assistant suggestion.**

A splint that prevents an injured person's condition from worsening **while another person remains physically present and actively stays with them**.

It does not heal.

It suspends deterioration through the literal narrative command:

**“Stay with me.”**

If the companion abandons the patient, the protection ends.

This could make rescue itself part of the magic rather than reducing medical treatment to an item effect.

**Narrative axis attacked:** deterioration / abandonment.

---

## Poultice of You've Had Worse

**Assistant suggestion.**

Works only upon someone who genuinely has survived a worse injury, illness, or ordeal.

The current injury is narratively compared against the worst thing in the person's history and downgraded accordingly.

Potentially powerful for extremely experienced or traumatized characters, which is also what makes it dangerous and strange.

The item essentially argues:

**You've survived worse than this. Therefore this cannot be what kills you.**

It could become disturbingly effective when used on someone with an exceptionally horrible past.

**Narrative axis attacked:** comparative severity.

---

## Bell of Walk It Off

**Assistant suggestion.**

Allows an injured person to function as though the injury were something they could simply “walk off.”

The catch is literal:

**the effect lasts only while they continue moving.**

If they stop walking, the injury fully asserts itself again.

Useful for:

* escaping danger,
* reaching shelter,
* crossing hostile territory,
* keeping someone mobile long enough to obtain real treatment.

It does not heal the injury.

**Narrative axis attacked:** temporary incapacity.

---

## Brooch of Scar Tissue

**Assistant suggestion.**

Makes the body behave as though an injury is **older than it actually is**.

A fresh wound becomes partly healed. The Brooch essentially advances the injury one stage into its future rather than completely restoring the body.

Possible progression:

fresh wound → closing wound
new burn → healing burn
recent fracture → partially knitted fracture

This overlaps somewhat with the Potion, but the conceptual distinction is that the Potion advances the patient's recovery to “morning,” while the Brooch ages the **injury itself**.

**Narrative axis attacked:** age of injury.

---

## Spoon of You Need to Eat Something

**Assistant suggestion.**

Food eaten with the Spoon becomes narratively restorative.

It does not transform food into a conventional healing potion. Instead, the meal becomes **the meal after which recovery begins**.

Particularly appropriate for:

* exhaustion,
* blood loss,
* illness,
* shock,
* prolonged deprivation.

The effect rests on one of the oldest forms of practical caretaking:

**“You need to eat something.”**

Works best if chicken soup is what is being eaten. Only mothers know why.

**Narrative axis attacked:** recovery threshold.

---

# Current Strongest Healing Set

The three user-created items form an especially clean core because they attack completely different properties of injury:

**Wrap of Not As Bad As You Thought**
Changes **how bad the injury actually is**.

**Potion of Here Take This And You'll Feel Better By Morning**
Changes **when recovery occurs**.

**Censer of I Feel Fine**
Changes **what everyone else believes about the injured person's condition**.

The strongest additional candidates are probably:

**Stitch of Not Yet**
Changes whether the injury is allowed to reach **its ending**.

**Cup of You'll Laugh About This Later**
Changes the injured person's relationship to **the emotional aftermath**.

Together, these create a distinctly Cynn theory of medicine:

**severity, time, perception, finality, and trauma.**

None simply says:

**“Heal 20 points.”**

And the interaction between the Potion and Bundle of Sunrise establishes an important larger principle of Cynn artifacts:

**individual items obey narrow narrative rules, but clever combinations can produce effects their makers may never have explicitly intended.**

# Chapter Five: The Devil You Don't Know

Now where was I?

Oh yes. The machinations of devilry.

So now we needed to approach this very carefully. So what did we do? Quill, on his day off, prayed. To his favorite devil. Mammon.

You don't know Mammon? That's fine. He's an archdevil. Probably the nicest one. Certainly the easiest one to get along with if you're willing to part with your valuables. He represents one vice... Greed.

You see, Quill may not look like it, but he's always scheming. Looking to benefit from his... situation. And that's what Mammon does best. "Help me help you..." is Mammon's favorite phrase. And you're helping him more than he helps you. But I digress.

Now, why would Mammon even care? Here's where it gets interesting. You see, Mammon has his own secrets. His own vaults. Vaults that Geryon is _dying_ to get into as well. Let's say, they're not friends. More like hated, vicious, pernicious enemies. That's putting it mildly.

Mammon doesn't just answer to anybody though. You see, Mammon hands out prayers to his followers. But not for free.

Prayers like these, the ones that seek Mammon's help, are given to the Prayer Auction on Erebus. Various underlings gather at the auction and bid on leads from prayers to prospects. Gate happened to be listening, judged the room, and put in a bid. I think he said he bid three Avernite imp-futures. Maybe you understand infernal economics better than me.

So _Gate_ was given the permission to respond, so he was the one who answered. He smelled opportunity. _Real opportunity_.

That's when I got introduced to my first Cornugon, a horned devil to you. He was already morphing... another few hundred years and a few more tasty scores, and he'd be in line for a promotion. His wings were especially grand... they were the body part that most screamed "pit fiend". He was well on his way, because he was way smarter than your average Cornugon.

Now Gate's presence in my little tent was certainly shocking, but not _that_ shocking. He _vooped_ in and announced himself. Not every devil has the ability to _gate_ in from Hell by themselves.

"I TOOK OUT A CONSIDERABLE LOAN TO PAY MAMMON FOR PLANAR TRAVEL PRIVILEGES. TO GATE INTO AND OUT OF GOLARION AT WILL IS EXPENSIVE."

My dear Gate, where would you be if you hadn't taken out that loan? Still be in some stuffy vault in Erebus. And I'm so glad you call it "gate" to and from places... I'd hate for your byname to be "Voop".

Anyway, Quill introduced himself. I think he was shaking in awe. He may have even peed a little.

Now Mop, don't start making fun of Quill. You'd pee yourself too if Socothbenoth suddenly showed up to give you the spanking you deserve. Stop your pee-pee-dance and sit in the corner until my story's done.

Now, where was I?

Oh yes. Rezzen was there. So Gate and Rezzen met. And we all shared the evidence. And Gate told him what needed to be done.

Deliver the tablet to Geryon. But, don't make it _look like_ he delivered the tablet to Geryon. In other words, make him fight for it. And _win_.

Pick your jaws up off the ground. You heard me right. Gate had already figured out who the timing mattered to, and why.

You see, Gate did a little digging. He has an Erinyes devil sidekick... let's call her Wings. She's really good at infiltration, kicking down doors, and kicking a little ass when needed. She's Gate's right-hand she-devil.

She learned that Geryon was quietly pulling together his private bodyguards for a big move. They were just waiting on one last detail of something. Don't ask me how she figured that out. She must have connections. She might be part succubus. Scratch that, I don't think she has a seductive bone in her body.

So, not only was Geryon planning to open the Feznark vault, but it looked like he was going to be present when he did.

To claim credit for it. To gloat. But, that would leave him vulnerable, to appear on the surface of Golarion with only a small retinue of pit fiends for protection. That showed real confidence.

That's when Mammon received a request from Geryon. To send a representative to Kaer Maga on a moment's notice. To witness a grand event. The culmination of Geryon's power.

Mammon told Gate about it.

"THAT MADE ME HAPPY."

Stop interrupting!

Now, what was _really_ interesting is that Mammon also knew that the big "A" didn't try to put a stop to it. Whatever it was.

Ordinarily, if the big "A" was about to be one-upped by one of the other archdevils and had to send a representative to witness the event, he would stop it posthaste.

But he didn't. And that, honeydew, may have been the biggest clue of all.

So Gate suggested Rezzen take the tablet back to the temple and wait for Droskar's agents to come take it for Geryon. Gate arranged for Droskar to know what Rezzen had found.

Now, you're probably asking why Geryon didn't just take the tablet by force himself. You see, if he did, he wouldn't be the Devil of Secrets. He'd just be the Devil of Kicking-Down-A-Door-And-Taking-Tablets. He had an ego the size of the Stygian Hell, and he wanted to prove he could manipulate the situation to his favor like a master of secrets should.

Rezzen needed help, but it needed to be not-so-obvious infernal help. He needed to put up a good fight, and only lose enough to believably retreat. Without the tablet.

Gate gave Rezzen a pair of bracers. Not just any bracers.

You see, he couldn't just send Wings with him. She'd stick out like a griffon at a zebra party. And give away the fact that he was receiving infernal help. Which would have tipped Droskar and Geryon off, potentially.

Instead, he enchanted those bracers with Wings herself. He put an Erinyes _inside_ the bracers. To learn. To help. And to kick ass.

She only agreed to it because she might learn a new way of fighting. _From the inside_. She's always looking for new ways to get an edge.

She hated being trapped in those bracers. Let's just say it wasn't your average heroic duo. She also hated being told to lose.

So Rezzen went back with the tablet and the bracers. At first, Rezzen said, it didn't go well. She countermanded some of his moves. They fought each other as much as their enemies. But they worked it out.

While they were down in the Underdark barely losing to Droskar's army of thug-slaves, I had a visit from my favorite investigative reporter. Let's call him Deadline.

Deadline is from the land of worshippers of Zon-Kuthon, Nidal. Your average Kuthite loves to be shaven bald, pierced, and adorned with black leather and spiked chains. Deadline looks more like a professor. And he has a head of long grey hair.

Deadline works for a periodical called _Sufferer's Digest_. Maybe you've heard of it. They publish "feel-good" stories about pain, trauma, and suffering. You know, to lift the spirits of those weirdos who are into that sort of thing.

And the country of Nidal is filled with them. Paying customers, all looking for ways to find enjoyment in commiserating with people half way around Golarion. And when I say commiserate, I mean... by choice. They do _things_ to themselves that most people pay good coin _not to have done to themselves_.

But then again, who am I to judge?

When Deadline walked in this time, it was clear he had been listening too much. He was writing in his magic journal, the one he and his editor write to each other in. One writes, the other sees it wherever they are.

"Miss Diddi, can I get you on the record about how you feel, dooming all of Golarion to a painful agonizing death at the hands of a Great Old One?"

That's a _real_ problem. We really did need a venue change what with the tent being so easy to listen through, now that I think about it.

I said, "I need you to hold this story. Trust me."

"If we're all doomed to die, what's the point of delaying the story?"

"Because I don't think we're doomed."

He seemed somewhat disappointed. I think he should write a piece about _his own disappointment_. I think he wanted to be doomed. Not to die, but to experience _the feeling_ of certain death coming. He should write a special on himself, while he's at it.

He said, "I'll stay near but I've got a deadline for publication. I'm going with the story in two days unless I hear something new from you."

Fair enough.

So, finally, Rezzen walked back through my tent. And all he said was, "Tomorrow."

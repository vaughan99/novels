# Chapter One: My Last Case

“Are you speaking to me?” The man in the chair asked me, but then looked up nervously.

They all do that. Eventually. I won't mind if you do.

Some do it immediately. Some last an entire reading before curiosity got the better of them. One woman had managed to last until the sand-glass was half empty.

Quill's sand-glass. After he took over as shop manager, it was the very first thing he requisitioned. He lives his life by it. Obsessed with writing in his ledgers. Obsessed with time. "Six fingers-wide of sand past the third bell," he might write in his ledger. My time is judged in centuries, not grains of sand.

Then his eyes came back down to me.

I smiled. I always smile. I love their reactions. I read them... _before_ I read them.

But no, _I_ didn't say a word. Not yet.

His eyes looked at Quill, nose-deep into his ledger. Quill shook his head and said, "Who else would I be speaking to? You must not have heard my question, young man. I'll pose it again. Do you have... a _reservation_?”

The client looked up, then looked at me, then back at Quill.

Quill was perched on his stool behind the side counter, spectacles balanced on the end of a nose much too small to support them properly. He did not need them.

This was obvious the moment he requisitioned them. Quill insisted they would make him look professional. They made him look like an imp wearing spectacles.

“Do you have an appointment?!” he asked again, his tone growing more impatient.

The client blinked. “I’m already sitting here.”

Quill stared at him, not bothering even to let his eyes see _through_ the lenses. He looked over them... probably for effect.

The client swallowed. “Ummm, no.”

Quill dragged one claw down the page. “Correct. You don't.”

The man looked at me.

I smiled. “You’ll get used to him.”

“I will not,” Quill said.

“I was talking _to the client_, Quill. He'll get used to you.”

The client shifted in the chair.

Up again. There it was. That little glance.

So now, take a moment. Look up. Get a good look. Let's get that out of the way.

Ready to continue? Good.

He swallowed. Then he looked at me.

“Now,” I said. “What did you bring?”

He reached into his coat and withdrew a handkerchief.

I stared at it.

He stared at me.

Quill scratched on the page and mumbled at the same time. "One handkerchief."

From somewhere underneath the table came the soft scrape of something being dragged across stone.

I held out my hand. “That’s it?”

The man placed the handkerchief in my palm. “It’s hers.”

“Whose?”

“My wife’s.”

I rubbed the cloth between two fingers.

Fine linen. Expensive. Perfumed.

Lavender.

I was actually muttering those words. What made me realize it was hearing Quill's scratching on the page, capturing every detail.

Something bitter underneath.

Not poison.

Fear, perhaps.

More scratching.

Fear has a scent if you spend enough time around it.

People deny this. People deny many obvious things.

I asked, “How long has she had it?”

“Years.”

“Are you sure?”

“Am I sure?”

“Men don't notice those things.”

He narrowed his eyes. "You'd know that because you've been... married?"

I laughed. "No, honeydew. I know that because every man I've ever known looks right past that bit of linen to their wife's..."

Quill's pen broke. That sometimes happens when he is writing too fast. He reached under the desk. I think he keeps over a hundred perfect goose feather pens under there.

The client went pale.

“Fine, I _think_ she's had it a long time.”

He relaxed.

Underneath the table, something snickered.

“Mop,” Quill said.

Silence.

“Mop!”

A wet little voice answered from somewhere near the floor.

“What?”

“Stop lurking beneath the client.”

“I’m cleaning.”

“You have been cleaning the same square of floor for... one finger-wide of sand.”

“Stubborn stain. Don't want client shoes to get stained, do we?”

The client drew his feet together. Slowly.

Very slowly.

Voop. A small green head appeared out of thin air from beneath the edge of the table.

Mop grinned at the client. Those teeth always get me. The teeth smile at you.

You heard me. The _teeth_... they smile. At you. Disturbing.

The client recoiled.

Mop was holding something pink between two fingers.

He popped it into his mouth. He chewed. The smile never left his face.

The client stared.

I sighed. “Mop.”

“What Miss Diddi?”

“Stop eating in front of customers.”

Mop swallowed. “That wasn’t eating.”

“What was it?”

“Cleaning.”

Quill made a note in the ledger. “That’s coming out of your pay.”

“We don’t get paid rune-slinger! Now shut your ink-hole.”

“Someday.” Quill tapped the page. “Then I’ll have it here.”

Mop narrowed his eyes. “I’ll chew you next.”

Voop.

Voop.

Both disappeared.

The client stared at the empty stool.

Then at the empty patch of floor.

Then at me.

I sighed. “Grow up, you two.”

Mop’s invisible voice drifted from the other side of the room.  “Feral.”

Quill retorted, “There is no such thing as a feral imp.”

“Feral imp.”

“I object to the taxonomy.”

“Jealous feral imp.”

Silence.

I looked at the client. He had gone very still.

"Never let Hell and Abyss into the same shop. Hell will nitpick you, and Abyss will pick every nit until you bleed. Somewhere in between work gets done, though, so it's not a total loss."

That got half a laugh out of him.

Better. Readings are easier when clients are relaxed.

I set the handkerchief on the table. That's when I noticed Mop had forgotten one of his jobs. He's always forgetting things.

"Mop, the basin?" I said.

"'Mop' this, 'Mop' that..."

The standard wooden basin floated through the air and landed on the table. "You're lucky I'm not on strike."

I smiled at the customer. "Now price. You're a shop clerk?"

"Peddler. I have a cart, and..."

I cut him off. I'm reasonable on price. It all depends on... what it depends on.

"What kind of peddling?" I said.

"What?"

"Toys, crossbows, miniature dragons, cookies? What do you peddle?"

"Used items."

"Junk."

"Some say junk."

Mop cut in. "Junk! I love junk! Sharp junk... pointy junk... bloody junk..."

Not helpful.

I came up with a price on the spot. "Two silver. On the table."

"You said the first taste was free."

"It is honeydew, but I already know you'll want a second question. Now, you want to know about your wife.”

He put two silver on the table.

Quill scratched a note in his ledger. You can always tell Quill's excitement when payment is offered by the pressure of the quill on the page. He sounded especially 'scratchy' today.

“Yes," replied the customer.

“You want to know if she’s unfaithful.”

He sat motionless.

I leaned toward him.

“Honeydew, you're carrying your wife's handkerchief and there's no blood anywhere. That all points to infidelity.”

The client rubbed his palms against his knees. “I want to know... No... I _need_ to know.”

I picked up the knife. He watched the blade.

Good.

Everyone watches the blade.

It gives them something to focus on besides the fact that I’m about to do something they’ll pretend later wasn’t nearly as bad as it was.

He swallowed.

I placed the tip of the knife against my belly. The client leaned back. That's usually for the best, because the next part stains.

Quill reappeared on his stool.

Mop reappeared behind the client looking over his shoulder. The client was too mesmerized to care.

Mop grinned. And panted. His little claws grasped the client's shoulders. He started jumping up and down on the chair-back in anticipation.

I pushed the blade in.

The first drop hit the basin.

Mop looked down.

Quill looked down.

The client stopped breathing.

I drew the knife sideways.

Gore spilled over my fingers.

Clients expect me to scream. But have you noticed how much scar tissue I have there, on my belly? I think I've lost feeling from my armpit to my beltline.

Anyway, the client made enough noise for both of us.

“Oh... GODS!”

Then he thought. "Oh... GODS!" That's good... When what they think and what they say are the same thing, that usually means they're not guilty, conspiring, or just generally untrustworthy.

I opened myself wider.

I can read the future, the past, the present, wherever the focus takes me. The intestines help me follow it.

_Not_ my poop. _That_ would be disgusting. What I have is more... elegant. Like bloody art. A fine painting. Made of gore.

Smells like... you guessed it... honeydew.

There are diviners who use cards. Bones. Stars. Smoke. Tea leaves.

Cowards.

I reached inside.

The client looked away.

“No.” I smiled.

He looked back.

“Sorry?”

“This is your reading. You participate.”

His face had gone gray. I grabbed his hands. I have to, suddenly, otherwise they all pull away. Well, the repeat customers rarely present their hands to me to begin with.

“You’re watching. And now... you're feeling... HERE. Feel that?”

He watched. He felt. He vomited.

Mop was there to catch it with the black bucket. He likes to show off like that, catching vomit in mid-hurl.

I moved a loop of intestine aside.

Mop leaned closer.

“Appreciate from farther away.”

He shuffled back. I could hear the frown on his face.

I closed my eyes.

Lavender.

Linen.

A woman’s hands.

A silver comb.

A bedroom window.

A man laughing.

Not the husband.

Was the woman, now a man.

Slinking in the shadows.

Drawing a knife.

The husband leaned forward. He wiped his chin, forgetting he had my blood on his hands. That's good... spittle on the chin can be so trance-breaking. My blood on his chin felt more... artistic.

“Well?” he said.

I looked down again.

The gore switched the vision.

Adultery wasn't there.

Very relieved. Common things are boring.

There was something else.

New loop of intestine. Starting there. Seasons change.

Something _it_ feared more than being discovered in the shadows of a dark alley.

Wait.

_It?_

Something beneath the obvious secret. Sneaking out of the house at night clearly wasn't the juiciest secret.

I reached deeper, but I sensed Mop's teeth smiling at me again.

"Mop, shut your teeth."

Quill wasn't helpful. "I think you meant shut your mouth."

Mop pointed at him. “Infernal crack-tosser! Shut your ink-hole.”

Quill’s eye twitched.

I found the vision again. Different. A place.

There.

Not a lover.

A meeting.

Three people.

No, four.

The husband spoke. “Diddi?”

I ignored him.

A wine cellar. Fake identities.

Laughing. A toast. To fooling the humans.

Back to their borrowed lives. Their boring lives.

Laughing inside.

I let go. He let go.

The husband leaned closer. “My wife?”

I began putting myself back together. This part has to be done quickly... you know, because my skin is already pulling back together at the edges. I once made the mistake of leaving the organs out... well, we don't need to get into that.

“Yes.”

His face collapsed.

“She is?”

“Unfaithful?”

He nodded.

“No.”

He blinked. “No?”

“Not in the way you mean.”

“What does that mean?”

“It means your marriage has bigger problems.”

“What kind?”

“First question was free.”

Quill smiled. He enjoys this part.

His mouth fell open. “I can pay more.”

“Of course you can.”

I cleaned my hands while Mop reached for a jar.

Very carefully, he began separating the blood. Mine went into the clean jar. Everything else went into the dark bucket.

He hummed while he worked.

The client watched him. “What’s the other bucket?”

Mop looked up. “Other liquids. Vomit. Bile. Melon rinds. _The bloooood of my victims!_”

The client stared.

I smiled. “Mop, tell the nice man we don’t have any victim blood.”

Mop looked at the client with a nasty smile. “Yesterday, there was this orc...”

Quill closed his eyes. “Mop.”

“What?”

“Cause mayhem on your own time.”

Mop grinned. “Fat imp on your stool. You have no life. You envy mine.”

“I am quite content doing clerical work.”

Mop tilted his head. Then he looked at the client.

"Tell the fat imp to shut his ink-hole. Or I go on strike."

He wasn't done pouring the blood into the jar yet. Going on strike in that moment would have been... messy.

Quill hesitated. “Fine. Your life is _interesting_.”

Mop was almost done pouring. “Bloooooody blood. Drippity drip. Oooh, another flesh-nugget.”

Mop popped another bit of pink into his mouth and chewed it slowly as he laid the basin down and capped the jar. He placed the jar on the shelf. He took the basin away for its own _private_ cleaning.

Quill murmured as he scratched in his inventory ledger, “One jar of Diddi's troll blood. Check. At least the landlord appreciates me.”

From somewhere far deeper in the shop, a voice rolled through the stone. “I appreciate silence.”

Mop giggled as he handed the client a towel.

I smiled. “Now. How badly do you want to know what your wife is _really_ hiding?”

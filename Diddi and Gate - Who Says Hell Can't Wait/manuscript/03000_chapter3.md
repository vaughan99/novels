# Chapter Three: Fame Comes Knocking

Anyway, _that's when_ the duergar entered the tent. I barely looked at him.

"Ascetics typically go to Greenhalo. She'll tell you about your afterlife."

Typical. Monks and their questions. "Am I on the right path? Have I sacrificed my _entire life_ for some meaningless soul journey? Or should I have listened to my father and be an accountant."

Don't tell Quill I said that.

Are any of you monks, honeydew? It's hard for these old eyes to tell. Well, if you are, no offense.

But the duergar just stood there, staring at me.

I repeated. "Greenhalo. Two tents down. Afterlife."

He kept staring.

That's when I realized he might actually want something from me. I looked at him again. Duergar? Don't see many of those outside of... well outside of the inside of a cavern.

He stepped forward. He looked fit. Young. Sure of himself. He didn't want to know about the afterlife. Now we're getting somewhere.

Quill started. "Ahem. Name and occupation?"

"Rezzen Shadowgit. Pledge of Magrim."

Now, Magrim is the god of the dwarven dead. He obsesses about unresolved blood feuds, mispelled runes on grave markers, and chronicling the length of men's beards when they die. Or something like that.

Quill's eyes grew wide as he scratched it on the page.

"Sit down," I said. "What's troubling you?"

Rezzen looked me in the eye for a cold moment. "I'm looking for an organization. The Brothers of the Seal."

I never had much occasion to think of the Brothers before that moment. Never came up. They apparently kept bickering a mile below Kaer Maga over a stupid door. Open the door. Close the door. Pick one. They fought wars over it.

I frowned.

"Do you have anything of theirs? Not sure how much I can tell you if I'm doing this reading cold."

He shook his head.

Damn. We'll have to do this the hard way.

"What do you need _from them_? Maybe we can get a focus around your need instead of something they actually owned. You know, approach the problem sideways?"

"I can't tell you."

Well, that was utterly unhelpful.

"Who sent you?"

"I can't tell you."

"What can you tell me?"

"I used to work for Droskar. I was tasked with finding out how to break another seal... the seal to Torag's vault. Droskar wanted to steal ideas from his inventions. Copy them. That's when Magrim found me."

Oooh, spicy.

"Was one of the grave markers mispelled? Did you forget your beard measuring tape?"

He sat there unsmiling. No loosening up this guy.

"Magrim found you? You call yourself a pledge of Magrim now?"

Behind Rezzen I heard furious scratching in Quill's ledger. I shook my head at Quill. "Off the record."

Quill grumbled, ripped the page and crumpled it. He dipped it in a can of bacon grease he kept on the far edge of his desk. He threw it at Mop, who promptly ate it.

"So Magrim _sent_ you."

Rezzen thought hard on the answer. Monks always think hard before they answer any question.

"I... suppose."

"Was it a vision, an avatar, or did he come to you personally?"

I love asking questions where I suggest that one of the gods might have actually visited a mortal.

"Avatar."

Damn. Foiled again. I always wanted to meet a god, just to say I'd met one.

"Did this avatar leave anything behind?"

"No."

Damn.

I looked at him again. His robes had a spot of blood. Haha.

"That spot of blood. You beat someone to a bloody pulp. Who was it?"

He didn't even look down. "Assassins of Droskar. They didn't take kindly to me leaving his employ."

"Your former buddies?"

"They were _not_ my friends. After a brief interrogation, they said their next orders after killing me were to visit the Brothers of the Seal. They did not survive the interrogation."

"Did you happen to save anything of _theirs_?"

You know, sometimes I get lucky. If I approach the sideways angle from yet another sideways angle, sometimes the two 'sideways' cancel each other out. But most of the time they just confuse the hell out of everyone involved. Me included.

And this is where he surprised me. He produced a key. It looked strange. A hundred teeth. Mithral.

Quill even left his stool to come take a closer look. He brought his ledger and was about to draw it. I shook my head again. He went back to his stool, dejected.

"That's something I can work with. Let's talk price."

He stared at me.

How do I come up with a price for a duergar monk of Magrim who used to be a duergar monk of Droskar, the master of endless toil and stolen ingenuity?

"The key is the price. Let me keep the key."

"Unless your vision proves that I need it."

Ooooh, a gamble. Love it.

"Done."

So now you know what comes next. The wood basin, the inevitable bickering, yadda yadda. But.

I opened myself up. I reached for his hands. And he lunged in, both hands. No vomit. His eyes were fixed on the gore. He was strong. I think that helped with the vision.

Rezzen's mind? Like steel. A focused blade. Sharp, but not in a _smart_ sense.

Assassins. Duergar. Wow, six-on-one. Rezzen, well done.

Gloating, but only for a moment.

The key.

No notes.

The key.

The key.

Backwards.

They took the key.

Another target.

A gnome.

The gnome stole the key.

Stole it.

From a dragon hoard. Sat there hundreds of years.

Forgotten.

Backwards.

Carried by a man into a dragon's den.

Looking for secrets.

Forbidden secrets.

Snake emblem.

Oh no, dragon.

Eaten.

Dragon stared at key.

Threw it onto the hoard.

I lurched back to reality.

"You're looking for a snake emblem. A previous owner of the key carried one. Several centuries ago."

I described the emblem. It was an amulet, probably religious.

"Bring me one of those medallions, and if it's connected, we'll know a lot more."

"Sounds like I still need the key. Might need it to open a door. Might need it for bait."

Quill opened the Escrow ledger and made an entry. "We shall keep it in escrow. It is not decided yet whether you shall need it or not. Now, description? I can't write 'off the books'. How about 'indeterminate object with teeth... no... protrusions.' Sounds specific but not too specific."

Quill took the key and placed it in his chest, next to old man Herbert's silver spoons that we were keeping in escrow too. These were the days before we had vaults.

Hey, we had to start somewhere!

That's when Shoes came back. Three melons. That didn't cost a full silver piece. Smart boy. He kept the change.

I said, "Those four coppers in your pocket. Consider that your tip."

He looked at me shyly. Yeah, I know what you did. Takes balls kid, real balls.

Shoes placed the basket near my chair, and asked if there was anything else. There's always something else.

"I need you to ask the beggars about the Brothers of the Seal. Quietly. Tell them you're trying to settle a bet. Whether they actually exist."

He nodded and left.

Rezzen asked for something to clean his hands. Mop jumped to his side and offered his tongue.

Quill took to wing and hovered over Mop, his stinger exposed.

"Mop!"

"Well..."

"No licking the customers. No touching the customers. No looking at the customers." Quill was irate even by Quill's standards.

Voop.

This is what it looks like when Mop goes on strike.

I handed Rezzen a clean, slightly moist towel. I kept those on hand.

I don't remember what I used to moisten them that day.

We'll add it to the laundry pile later. Another job I can give Shoes... laundry. I could finally afford laundry then. That felt luxurious.

The next part I really hated, but if you want repeat business, sometimes you have to send your client to somebody else.

"Remember how I said you needed to speak to Greenhalo, two tents down? Well, she _might_ be able to give you the goods on that medallion."

His face gave no expression. He just stood up.

No thanks. He just left.

Quill opened his exit ledger. "Diddi, how should I characterize the end of the session?"

"Success. Failure. Write both."

"Hmmmm. I have no way to record both."

"What?"

"The space is a checkbox. Check for success. Ex for failure."

"Invent a new mark."

"Hmmmm. Circle? Circle. Implies something to be filled later."

Mop reappeared.

"Off-strike. Needed to witness the fat imp getting perplexed." He giggled.

"Feral creature."

"Jealous."

The next day, Blood-Drop the bloatmage... we call him that because you never know when he'll drop by, looked in on the shelf of troll blood. He seemed especially _needy_.

Bloatmages are like regular mages who've figured out that some dweomers are right there in the blood. So they flood their blood vessels. They use leeches too, sucking off the excess before it stains their silk shoes.

The bright side: that extra blood _really_ gives their spells some kick. The down side: overdosing turns them into drunken raging spell-barbarians. Or something like that. Is barbarian still a word, honeydew? I forget.

Quill started in. "Do you have a reservation?"

Blood-Drop ignored him and wallowed over to the jars. His leeches were plugging themselves into a hole that wept blood from his skin. They were barely keeping up.

Mop eyed them anxiously. "Fall off. Just one. Pweeeeez!"

Quill shook his head. "Sir, I asked if you have an appointment."

Blood-Drop finally acknowledged Quill. But not in the way he liked.

"Your inquisition will cease, infernal minion. You know who I am. I must not delay. The power is calling."

His attention turned back to the jars.

Quill pulled out the Diddi blood ledger, accounts receivable.

"How many do you need?"

Blood-Drop grabbed one of the shelf. Apparently he already had a needle in his neck. Didn't bother to ask. He just hooked it up to the jar.

My blood flowed in. Wow. Didn't know my blood was all that powerful.

His blood vessels screamed... at least I thought I heard screaming.

"BEDSHEETS! THE BEDSHEETS ARE DIRTY!"

Oh no. That's real trouble. He was hallucinating. He knows my blood is powerful, but he can't resist taking a hit of the raw stuff. Didn't refine it. Didn't age it or prepare himself for it.

"FIREBALLS!!!! CHAMBERPOT... FIREBALLS!!!! MUHAHAHAHA!!!"

The next thing I saw was Mop. The leeches had fallen off, and he was eating them like carnival treats, watching the tent like the world was going to end. And he loved it.

So I reached over. I'm usually pretty gentle for a 7000-year-old troll. I worked too hard and cut myself too many times to be ended by an insane bloatmage. No sir.

I grabbed him by the throat. That shut him up quick.

He started waving his hands violently, casting his spells without a voice. I laughed. Shoulda learned metamagic, weirdo.

I told him, "You do know that I'm going to have to charge you extra this time? This is the second time this month we've had this conversation."

He looked at me wild-eyed. This may work in the dungeons and out in the Cinderlands maybe, but here in my shop? No way.

I wagged my finger at him. "No mainlining blood in the tent. Take it home. Take it to your enemy's house. But no using here. We're a respectable establishment."

Mop was picking his nose. Mop was not counted in that statement.

His excess blood was spewing forth. Well, his leeches had already given up, and so had half his blood vessels. He bled on my box of melons.

That made me... cross.

Don't make me cross.

But I let him bleed. It's the only way to handle one of these bloatmages until they get off their high.

I let him go.

"I cannot fathom why the vision of dirty bedsheets and chamberpots suddenly made me lose my wits. Your unfiltered sanguinity is most intoxicating... and strange. I almost..."

"Look, just no mainlining, got it honeydew? Now pay the human boy to get me a fresh batch of melons or I'll cut open a few new attachment sites for your leeches."

"Of course Miss Diddi."

He left me a purse for the blood, the jar, Mop's cleanup expenses (oh wait, those are free), and the ink that it cost for Quill to document everything.

Before he left, Blood-Drop reminded me why I liked him, despite all his nasty habits. "Shall I drop by tomorrow with my brother's latest line of alchemical wrinkle creams? He's worked on a new formula."

Now we're talking. Blood-Drop was such a smoothy sometimes. I almost forgot I nearly _ended him_.

"Three jars of wrinkle cream."

"Of course."

That brightened my mood. I know I'm old, but behind this old saggy troll exterior is a girl that just wants to be loved... by other... trolls... never mind. I just want to keep the skin folds off my other skin folds. Makes them hard to count.

Mmmm, time for another.

Mmmmm. Melon.

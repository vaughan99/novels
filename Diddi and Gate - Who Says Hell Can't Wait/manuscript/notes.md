## Droskar’s actual seal-breaking program

## Diddi & Gate as a reusable campaign/story engine

This emerged as one of the strongest broader ideas.

The basic engine is:

**small client problem → Diddi reads it → unexpected larger thread appears → field investigation → another object/secret comes back → Diddi reads again → larger structure emerges.**

The client may only care about the original small answer.

Diddi and the reader accumulate the larger case.

That makes old tabletop campaigns unusually easy to adapt because seemingly disconnected adventures, side quests and NPC encounters can all become different threads in Diddi’s investigative work.

You can adapt another campaign simply by asking:

**What client problem causes Diddi to pull the first thread?**

The engine also lets stakes reset between books. Book One can end with Asmodeus, Geryon, Mammon and the dwarven pantheon, while Book Two can start with somebody asking Diddi to find a missing goat.

The new case does not feel “smaller” because Diddi’s business was always about information, not progressively larger apocalypses.

## Modular field-party NPCs

Diddi & Gate naturally provides the DM—or novelist—with ready-made characters who can temporarily join an adventuring party without permanently bloating the core cast.

**Mop** is stealth, invisibility, surveillance, sabotage, chaos and the possibility that the scout creates an entirely new problem.

**Shoes** becomes the ordinary-human face, street intelligence, markets, social infiltration, errands, contacts and questions asked by someone nobody notices.

The exact slang can be revisited, but the business model is right.

### Flicker — The Fear-Eating Child

**Flicker** is Diddi’s byname for an otherwise ordinary twelve-year-old human girl whose soul merged at birth with that of a **wisp lord**. She is not possessed and there are not two beings inhabiting one body; Flicker is the fused result.

Physically and socially, she should remain essentially a **1st-level human child**. Her supernatural capabilities come from the wisp-lord side and can reach roughly **15th-level expression** under the right circumstances. The key design principle is that her body does not scale upward just because the wisp power becomes more accessible.

Flicker appears unusually fearless. She walks into graveyards, steps between bullies and their victims, and tends to move toward frightening situations rather than away from them. Part of that personality may simply be who she is, but her life experience reinforces it: **fear around her makes her stronger**. It does not matter whether that fear comes from friends, enemies, strangers, or people afraid of Flicker herself.

Her power should be framed as **electrical anomaly**, not weather control. She is deliberately unlike the Storm archetype. She does not command wind, rain, temperature, fog, or climate. At high power, the environment around her becomes electrically unstable because she is saturated with fear-fed wisp energy.

Her abilities develop by degree of access:

* At low access, touching her can produce an electrical shock.
* She can **blink** short distances.
* When blinking, she briefly becomes an electrical arc between origin and destination. If her path passes through a creature, she can attempt to hit it and discharge a **shocking-grasp-like effect** through the target.
* She can choose not to discharge when passing through allies.
* At higher access, the blink becomes longer and more deliberately offensive.
* At still higher access, she gains **dimension-door-scale teleportation**.
* At maximum access, she can effectively **become lightning**, combining long-range teleportation with a violent electrical strike. The high-end version was envisioned as roughly a dimension-door/lightning-bolt hybrid capable of dealing about **15d6 electricity damage in the same round**.
* Her highest expression also includes a powerful **static field** around her.
* At full wisp-lord access, she can create **lightning-storm-scale electrical phenomena**, though this should feel like an electrical catastrophe centered on Flicker rather than conventional weather control.

The visual language should emphasize static and conduction rather than storm-goddess imagery: hair rising, metal humming, arcs crawling over surfaces, ozone in the air, clothing clinging, tiny shocks between nearby people, and lightning traveling in unnatural directions—wall to floor, fence to gravestone, sword to sword, or through Flicker herself.

Her short-range teleportation has a playful side. When she is comfortable and having fun, Flicker may pretend she is a bullfighter, repeatedly blinking through opponents and yelling:

**“Olé!”**

with each pass.

This creates a potentially dangerous feedback loop: Flicker has fun, enemies become alarmed, their fear feeds her, she becomes stronger, and the game becomes increasingly unfair for whoever she is tormenting.

Her apparent fearlessness should therefore be treated as both personality and learned experience. Most children learn that frightening situations mean danger. Flicker has repeatedly learned:

**The more frightened everyone else becomes, the more capable I become.**

Her original connection to Diddi is that someone likely brought her in expecting Diddi to diagnose or remove a possession. Diddi discovered that there was nothing to exorcise. The girl and the wisp lord had already become a single soul at birth.

For campaign use, Flicker works especially well as a **former client/contact rather than a conventional combat NPC**. At low levels she can simply be the fearless local girl who knows graveyards, mausoleums, children’s rumors, and places adults overlook. In a later high-stakes disaster involving widespread panic, that same seemingly minor contact can suddenly become an extraordinarily powerful means of escape, infiltration, electrical destruction, or battlefield movement.

Mechanically, the intended approach is:

**Build the human as level 1. Treat the wisp-lord abilities as a separate supernatural power source with a ceiling around level 15. Fear controls access to those abilities; it does not make the human body itself higher level.**

#### Core concept

Deadline is a Kuthite journalist who travels in pursuit of exceptional suffering. He interviews survivors, examines wounds, documents atrocities and records the experience of pain with the attention of a scholar and the enthusiasm of a connoisseur.

His intended readers are Kuthite believers back in Nidal. His objective is not merely to describe terrible events but to reproduce their emotional and sensory impact through exceptionally accomplished prose, illustrations and illusion.

He is particularly interested in unusual forms of suffering. The stranger the circumstances, the more compelling the story.

Deadline follows a particular interpretation of his faith: death is cheating because it deprives the sufferer of further education.

Accordingly, he performs battlefield surgery when someone is in danger of dying. He does not necessarily intervene to eliminate pain; he intervenes to preserve the person experiencing it.

#### Appearance and physical characteristics

Deadline deliberately avoids the recognizable visual stereotypes of Kuthite devotees.

He is not bald, covered in piercings or draped in chains. He dresses in unadorned black, looking more like a traveling scholar or sober correspondent.

His principal devotional implement is a compression sleeve lined with inward-facing spikes, worn on one leg. Every day, he moves it to the other leg.

His physical build reflects his discipline:

* Extremely high Constitution and pain tolerance.

* Low Dexterity, particularly for mobility.

* Slow, deliberate movements rather than an agile fighting style.

#### Personality and performance

His primary acting reference is Count Rugen from The Princess Bride.

He approaches pain with intense intellectual curiosity rather than wild-eyed sadism. When faced with an extraordinary wound, he wants to know exactly how it happened, what it felt like, how the victim responded and whether the effects can be reproduced.

His manner should be methodical, courteous and disconcertingly enthusiastic. He can conduct a surprisingly ordinary conversation while examining an appalling injury.

He is not indifferent to whether a victim survives. He is deeply interested in survival—although his reasons may be horrifying to the victim.

The distinction is important: Deadline should not come across as a typical Kuthite cultist transplanted into the party. He is a professional journalist whose religion informs every aspect of his work.

#### His publication and reporting methods

Deadline writes for a Nidalese publication _Sufferer's Digest_,  with an editorial bent shaped by Kuthite values.

He is a master of three complementary reporting disciplines: prose, visual illustration and illusion-based reproduction of reality.

The illusions allow readers to experience a reconstruction of the events he reports, taking their reading far beyond the written word.

He submits his work to his editor through magical messaging. The same channel carries deadlines, assignments, editorial feedback and presumably complaints about his failure to meet a deadline while pursuing some irresistible new atrocity.

This arrangement allows a GM to introduce story hooks through either Deadline himself or an unexpected editorial assignment from Nidal.

#### Relationship with Diddi

Deadline has been Diddi's client longer than anyone else who still regularly visits her.

He often spends time near her tent, hoping to hear about an interesting reading. Diddi's clientele constitutes an endlessly replenishing source of potential stories.

When no current client presents anything sufficiently compelling, Deadline brings Diddi objects he has collected and commissions readings to discover where extraordinary suffering may occur—or where an object's history might lead him.

Their relationship is commercially and professionally complementary. Deadline wants stories, Diddi wants problems to solve, and both appreciate information that everyone else might consider worthless.

He also provides a natural link between Diddi and the wider world. A disaster occurring thousands of miles away can bring Deadline back to her door with a physical focus, an eyewitness account or an urgent editorial deadline.


#### Combat and medical role

Deadline is an experienced battlefield surgeon and expert in the physiological effects of pain. His high Constitution means he can endure extreme physical punishment, but his limited mobility makes him poorly suited to evasive fighting.

Acid is a possible signature weapon, chosen because he is interested in its capacity to produce prolonged and complicated injuries. That remains a proposed combat specialization rather than an established feature.

He operates according to a very particular medical ethic: if a wounded person is likely to survive, he may prefer to observe, interview and document them. If they are about to die, he intervenes surgically.

His presence can therefore create uncomfortable situations for adventurers. He might save an enemy combatant that the party intended to leave for dead, then insist on interviewing them.

In battle, he may choose not to engage unless death is emininent, choosing instead to witness everyone's movements and actions. He should have extraordinary tactical ability however, providing advanced suggestions on battlefield positioning, terrain, and exploiting weaknesses. He is a battlefield historian and can draw on that knowledge to help direct combat.

#### Using Deadline in a campaign

##### Level-independent encounter hooks

No fixed stat block needed; his role is driven by the story.

At level 1 — The battlefield correspondent

Deadline arrives following a violent incident, stabilizes a dying survivor and asks the party to help reconstruct what happened. His interview uncovers a contradiction that becomes the party's first investigation.

During an ongoing campaign — The unsolicited companion

Deadline hears that the party is heading somewhere especially dangerous. He wishes to accompany them because the story promises extraordinary suffering. He can contribute medical expertise, research and reporting, but his lack of mobility creates practical difficulties.

At higher levels — The extraordinary assignment

His editor commissions a report on a supernatural disaster, extraplanar battlefield or previously undocumented form of suffering. Deadline arrives with an object for Diddi to read and enlists the party to help him follow the resulting lead.

Recurring complication — The story isn't over

Deadline saves an important antagonist from death. He believes the survivor has considerably more to teach him, and that the experience would make excellent reading.

#### Points to develop later

Three aspects remain especially useful to decide during writing.

First, how does Deadline protect his sources? Given Diddi's developing policy of deliberately avoiding real names, a professional reporter from Nidal presents an interesting contrast. His editor presumably wants specifics, but his informants may have extremely good reasons to remain anonymous.

Second, does his magic reproduce only sights and sounds, or can his illusory reporting convey sensations of pain? The latter would make his publication a particularly distinctive Kuthite institution and explain why he wants meticulously accurate firsthand testimony.

Third, what does Deadline do when someone refuses to be interviewed? His commitment to journalistic accuracy might compete directly with his religious fascination, creating a useful distinction between an eccentric but welcome recurring ally and someone the party may eventually have reason to distrust.

#### NPC design principle

Deadline follows suffering, not the player characters. He can join an adventure, depart when a more compelling story appears, and return years later carrying a fresh mystery for Diddi. His medical abilities and obsessive reporting provide utility at level 1, while his contacts and interest in extraordinary suffering remain relevant through level 20.


### Urn — Magrimite Field Monk / Diddi Contact

**Urn** is Diddi’s byname for a younger monk of **Magrim’s order**, a dwarven religious institution focused on the dead, ancient mysteries, funerary obligations, unresolved deaths, and truths that survive their witnesses.

He is not Rezzen, but he comes from an order heavily shaped by Rezzen’s influence after the Geryon crisis.

#### Core role

Urn is a **junior mortuary investigator** and field monk.

He is useful when an adventure involves:

* disturbed graves,
* unidentified remains,
* disputed burials,
* missing funerary objects,
* ancestral claims,
* ancient battlefields,
* clan history,
* tomb architecture,
* old dwarven mysteries,
* or situations where the official account of a death does not match the physical evidence.

Diddi and Urn complement each other naturally:

**Urn understands the dead.
Diddi interrogates what they left behind.**

Urn can interpret bones, burial practices, clan markings, funerary law, ancient dwarven customs, and death-related evidence.

Diddi can take a connected object and trace ownership, handling, and events backward through its history.

Together they can solve cases neither could easily resolve alone.

#### Relationship with Diddi

Rezzen likely created the original bridge between Diddi and Magrim’s order.

Over time, the relationship became institutional because Diddi occupies a uniquely useful position for the Magrimites: she can resolve ancient mysteries involving the dead by reading objects associated with them.

A senior monk eventually sent Urn to Diddi on a case.

Urn expected some solemn ancient diviner.

Instead he got Diddi, Quill’s bureaucracy, Mop’s chaos, and whatever happened to be wandering through the tent that day.

Diddi solved the problem.

Urn became the official handler of Diddi from their perspective.

Their working dynamic can be summarized as:

**Urn brings context.
Diddi punctures ceremony.**

Typical interaction:

> “The deceased was High Thane—”
>
> “Bring me his belt buckle.”
>
> “The historical significance—”
>
> “Belt. Buckle.”

Urn respects Diddi’s results even if her irreverence can be exhausting.

#### The order’s culture

Magrim himself is Lawful Neutral, but this particular monastic order leans **Lawful Evil**.

The order became significantly harder under Rezzen’s influence.

Rezzen entered the original campaign already LE after years of service to Droskar. He abandoned Droskar, but he did **not** abandon everything Droskar had taught him.

He carried forward:

* tireless labor,
* extraordinary endurance,
* contempt for comfort,
* willingness to suffer,
* willingness to make others suffer,
* relentless pursuit of an objective,
* and a strong belief that ends often justify means.

He redirected all of that toward Magrim and toward preventing catastrophes like Geryon’s plot.

The institutional lesson became:

**You do not stop ancient conspiracies by becoming tired, squeamish, or sentimental halfway through.**

The order is therefore ruthless in pursuing truth.

They are not primarily sadistic in the Kuthite sense, but individual members may take some satisfaction in breaking resistance.

Their concern is not whether the process hurts.

Their concern is whether the truth is established and the larger danger is contained.

#### Core principles of the order

**The dead are owed the truth.**
False histories, concealed murders, stolen identities, improper burials, fraudulent ancestry, and falsified records are offenses against both Magrim and the dead.

**Truth outranks comfort.**
Embarrassment, reputation, emotional distress, secrecy, and sometimes physical suffering are secondary concerns.

**Do not stop because the work is unpleasant.**
This is part of Rezzen’s Droskarite inheritance.

**Do not kill a useful witness unnecessarily.**
Death ends questioning and can permanently destroy information.

**Ancient mysteries are potentially dangerous until proven otherwise.**
Rezzen lived through a case in which an obscure seal mystery nearly became a planetary catastrophe. That experience permanently shaped the order.

The post-Rezzen attitude is essentially:

**Resolve the dead before the dead become everyone else’s problem.**

#### Urn personally

Urn is younger and less hardened than Rezzen.

He does not need to be a miniature version of him.

Urn can be personable, calm, disciplined, and relatively easy to travel with while still carrying unsettling assumptions inherited from the order.

For example:

> “We should open the tomb.”
>
> “It has been sealed for three hundred years.”
>
> “Yes.”
>
> “And you’re fine with that?”
>
> “No. I’m fine with determining why somebody wanted it sealed.”

Urn treats corpses as **evidence**, not scenery.

Where other adventurers see a skeleton, he notices:

* burial posture,
* whether remains were moved,
* weapon marks,
* missing grave goods,
* clan identifiers,
* funerary preparation,
* whether kin performed the burial,
* whether rites were interrupted,
* and whether the official date or manner of death makes sense.

He should be methodical rather than gloomy.

Death is simply his field of work.

#### Party role

Urn is a strong **level-1-friendly companion NPC** because he contributes expertise without functioning as a bodyguard who solves combat for the party.

His useful areas include:

* Religion,
* Medicine,
* dwarven history,
* genealogy,
* funerary rites,
* tombs and burial architecture,
* battlefield remains,
* identifying corpses,
* ancient inscriptions,
* and disciplined defensive monk combat.

He can accompany a party because the **dead matter to the mission**, not because Diddi hired muscle.

A simple low-level hook might be:

> A family crypt has been disturbed. The Magrimites cannot determine whether it was robbery, desecration, or something more serious. Urn needs a few capable people to accompany him below Kaer Maga.

At high levels, the same role scales naturally:

> An ancient dwarven tomb contains an object whose provenance contradicts three thousand years of accepted history.

#### Urn versus Rezzen

Urn is the order’s normal campaign-facing contact.

If the party is powerful enough, or the problem is sufficiently serious, Diddi may send **Rezzen Shadowgit** instead.

By that point, Diddi starts calling Rezzen:

**Risen**

Whether Rezzen likes it is irrelevant.

The escalation is therefore:

**Urn** — ordinary field investigation.
**Rezzen / “Risen”** — this has become a serious Magrimite matter.

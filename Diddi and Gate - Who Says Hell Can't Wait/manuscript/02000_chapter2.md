# Chapter Two: Orientation

So if you're new here, welcome to Kaer Maga. The City of Strangers.

The city of the strange more like. Every corner has a secret. Which keeps me in business.

What's my business, really?

I'm a troll. I already said that. But not your average troll. Like the other Augurs. Funny how close that term is to "Ogres".

They hate me. Their divination comes as "grandiose". Self-important. Clueless really.

Want to win a war? Visit Torntusk. He'll give you a grand spectacle. Maps. Drawings. Legions of little lead figures he can move around the board.

More interested in the divine? Visit Greenhalo. She's in with all the gods' divinations. She can tell you everything you need to know to land in Elysium when the ghost gives up your shell. "When you see Pharasma, tell 'er Greenhalo sent ya." Yeah, right.

Nobody wanted to talk to the Augur that takes common iron or a couple of chickens for her tells. But that's what I do. I'm in it for the little guy... don't mean just halflings, neither. I mean the peasants. The soldiers. The... chambermaids. Ha. I like them.

Oh, I'm famished. Regenerating this often takes a toll... heehee... on a troll. Now where's that melon I had my eye on.

Mmmmmm. Slurp.

Now, where was I?

Oh yes.

What do I do?

I solve things. And I'm _really_ good at reading between the lines... of my bowels that is. That's how this divination works. It's all there for reading. And you sew yourself up, eat a melon, and... you get the picture.

I'm sure I'm not the first troll you've talked to. Unless, you're new to Kaer Maga. Or you love visiting the underside of bridges. But you don't look like a bridge-lover.

Wait. What's that you say? I didn't tell you how the last case played out? Oh, silly me. I must have gotten famished.

That case was slightly interesting. His wife was actually a doppelganger and a serial killer. Part of a cabal of doppelgangers. Don't worry, "she" wasn't going to kill him... not that you care anyway. And, the Duskwardens have been informed. They'll do something about it, I'm sure. If others in town don't do so first.

That's the thing about this town. Nobody's in charge, but everyone's in charge of their street. I mean, everyone who can handle problems themselves. Don't cross anybody, and you'll get along just fine here. Really. Cross someone, and well, you'll find out about street justice. Kaer Maga style.

What is that style? Well, we have the Church of the Abiding Vigilante. It's a new sect of the Church of Abadar. We have one of them on our street... I call him Gendarme.

You'll recognize him as the one with crossbow and cesti. If he sees locals arguing, he'll referee a fist-fight or maybe a drinking game to get commerce flowing again. Imagine two vampires locked in a drinking game, honeydew. You heard me.

But if he gets a complaint about a thief stealing, or a murder, he'll organize a posse. And he'll hunt you down.

They're quick and efficient. Don't cross the Abiding Vigilantes. They're all inquisitors. And they insist on order, especially because there's no law.

And now let me welcome you to our business: Diddi & Gate Investigative Divinations, Inc. That's the proper name. But if you just say "Diddi's" anywhere in town, they'll know what you mean.

Now, why do I have an imp and a quasit on staff you ask? Funny story that.

Back then I had different shop help... he was a halfling named Shoeless. He kept the books, took the payments, kept the tent clean, fetched things in market, and did some light investigative work. Brilliant halfling. Quiet and hard-working.

One day two hedge wizards came walking through my tent flap. They wanted me to settle a debt.

One had an imp familiar. The other had a quasit. You know where this is going, I'm sure.

They carried a wand in. They wagered their familiars as payment about who created the wand. They were convinced it was one of two factions of wizards. Don't remember which. Didn't matter anyhow.

Funny thing, that wand. It was a _wand of identify_, according to the runes on it. But it refused to identify itself, much less anything else. Aura was curious too. More transmutation, less divination.

So they had me do the reading.

Silly wizards. Turned out the wand had a childhood. In fact, it _was_ a child. A _mimic_ child, to be exact. The wizards argued. They threatened each other. They threatened me. They made me cross.

Don't make me cross.

Anyway, I knocked their heads together. They got headaches, and I got the two familiars. Shoeless disappeared the next day. And these two became my shop helpers. Maybe they conspired to make him disappear. Maybe they were bored. Maybe Shoeless feared for his life and fled. I'm not sure.

We put out an ad because... well, nobody wanted to sell melons to Mop. And Quill said that buying melons and cavorting with low-lifes who can't spell wasn't in his job description.

So then Shoes walked in. Well, he wasn't Shoes then. He was just a human boy, probably thirteen, maybe twenty-two. To a troll, they all taste the same.

Now, this was before... there was nothing to look up and see now, so he just sat there staring at me. Probably wondered if I was going to eat him. Or let the quasit eat him. I'm not like that.

"I... I... I'm here to run errands."

Quill started scratching in a new ledger. "Already knows the job description. Check."

I sized him up. Can he muscle through a crowd with a basket of melons? Yeah, I think so. Does he _look like_ a clueless kid who nobody would look at twice? Absolutely. Which is _exactly_ what I need.

"You're here to go to market and buy things for the shop. And occasionally, I'll have you ask questions of people you don't know."

The boy looked around the shop nervously. "Where's the one you called 'Mop'?"

He looked up. I forgot, in the old tent, Mop liked to hang from the tent poles. So yes, there was something to look up to there as well. Sorry if I'm confusing you.

Mop stared down at him. I think he might have been eating a cat. Fur fell to the floor.

"Mop, clean up the fur when you're done, or I'll put it in the ledger." Quill always made threats like that.

Mop just stared at the boy. The boy stared at him.

Mop dropped to the ground and threw the remains of his lunch in the black bucket. He then fetched a tablet where he had scratched things.

"Hey! Booger-bag! Tell the other booger-bags I do jobs. Good jobs!"

On the tablet were words and prices. "Jump-scare, one meat-slab. Hauntings, two meat-slab. _Stop_ hauntings, FOUR meat-slab! Vermin-hunting, free (until full)."

Quill rubbed his temples. Shoes just sat there, speechless.

I broke the tension. "One silver a day running my errands. Deal?"

The boy nodded, never taking his eyes off of Mop.

I liked this boy. He knew better than to turn his back on a quasit. Smart kid.

Quill turned the page on his ledger. "Alright new-face, I need your official name for the scroll."

The boy started to give his true name. I held up my hand.

"No true names for employees here, kid. True names are power. You want to keep your soul in your shirt around infernals? We use bynames only."

I like handing out names. It's like starting a whole new identity. The past can be so boring.

"Shoes. You're Shoes."

I flipped him two silver. One for his daily pay, and one for the sweet melons.

"Now, come back with ripe melons only. And ask Gertrude if the street thugs are still bothering her."

He couldn't wait to run to the market. And out of my tent. Did I mention he was a smart kid?

Now, what makes us so famous?

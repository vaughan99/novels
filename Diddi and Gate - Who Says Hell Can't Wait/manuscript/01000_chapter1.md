# Chapter One

I know what you’re thinking.

How can a troll be this eloquent? This well-spoken? This in-tune with the world around them?

I have a secret.

And it’s dying to come out.

But first, I’ll make a confession.

I actually do know what you’re thinking.

Or at least I will, after the first drops of blood hit the basin.

My price?

How about that elven cloak? That bauble you call a magic ring but we both know isn't? That tidbit of gossip involving the Lord of Magnimar’s wife?

Everything’s for trade, honeydew. Your future. Your husband’s past. Your enemy’s next mistake.

All I need is something of value and something to focus on.

Something they wore. Something they carried. Something they touched often enough to leave themselves behind.

There’s no such thing as worthless information.

And I’ll give you your first taste for free.

---

“Are you speaking to me?”

The man in the chair asked me, but then looked up nervously.

They all did that.

Eventually.

Some did it immediately. Some lasted an entire reading before curiosity got the better of them. One woman had managed to last until the sand-glass was half empty.

Quill's sand-glass. After he took over as shop manager, it was the very first thing he requisitioned. He lives his life by it. Obsessed with writing in his ledgers. Obsessed with time. "Six fingers-wide of sand past the third bell," he might write in his ledger. I'd just say where Kaer Maga's most famous new shadow fell on the lake's edge. That tells time well enough.

Then his eyes came back down to me.

I smiled. I always smile. I love their reactions. I read them... _before_ I read them.

But no, _I_ didn't say a word. Not yet.

“Who else would I be speaking to?”

His eyes looked at Quill, nose-deep into his ledger. "You must not have heard my question, young man. I'll pose it again. Do you have... a _reservation_?”

The client looked up, then looked at me, then back at Quill.

Quill was perched on his stool behind the side counter, spectacles balanced on the end of a nose much too small to support them properly. He had bought the spectacles himself. He did not need them.

This was obvious the moment he requisitioned them. Quill insisted they would made him look professional. They made him look like an imp wearing spectacles.

“Do you have an appointment?!” he asked again, his tone growing more impatient.

The client blinked. “I’m already sitting here.”

Quill stared at him, not bothering even to let his eyes see _through_ the lenses. He looked over them... probably for effect.

The client swallowed. “Ummm, no.”

Quill dragged one claw down the page.

“Correct. You don't.”

The man looked at me.

I smiled.

“You’ll get used to him.”

“I will not,” Quill said.

“I was talking _to the client_, Quill. He'll get used to you.”

The client shifted in the chair.

Up again.

There it was. That little glance.

He swallowed. Then he looked at me.

Good.

“Now,” I said. “What did you bring?”

He reached into his coat and withdrew a handkerchief.

I stared at it.

He stared at me.

Quill scratched on the page and mumbled at the same time. "One handkerchief."

From somewhere underneath the table came the soft scrape of something being dragged across stone.

I held out my hand.

“That’s it?”

The man placed the handkerchief in my palm.

“It’s hers.”

“Whose?”

“My wife’s.”

I rubbed the cloth between two fingers.

Fine linen. Expensive. Perfumed.

Lavender.

I was actually muttering those words. What made me realize it was hearing Quill's scratching on the page, capturing every detail.

Something bitter underneath.

Not poison.

Fear, perhaps.

More scratching.

Fear has a scent if you spend enough time around it.

People deny this.

People deny many obvious things.

“How long has she had it?”

“Years.”

“Are you sure?”

“Am I sure?”

“Men don't notice those things.”

He narrowed his eyes. "You'd know that because you've been... married?"

I laughed. "No, honeydew. I know that because every vision of every man I've ever laid eyes on looked right past that bit of linen to their wife's..."

Quill's pen broke. That sometimes happens when he is writing too fast. He reached under the desk. I think he generally keeps over a hundred perfect goose feather pens under there.

The client went pale.

“Fine, I _think_ she's had it a long time.”

He relaxed.

Behind him, something snickered.

“Mop,” Quill said.

Silence.

“Mop!”

A wet little voice answered from somewhere near the floor.

“What? He he he...”

“Stop lurking beneath the client.”

“I’m cleaning. Mmmm...”

“You have been cleaning the same square of floor for one finger-wide of sand.”

“Stubborn stain. Don't want client shoes to get stained, do we?”

The client drew his feet together.

Slowly.

Very slowly.

Voop. A small green head appeared out of thin air rom beneath the edge of the table.

Mop grinned at the client. Those teeth always get me. The teeth smile at you. The _teeth_... _smile_!

The client recoiled.

Mop was holding something pink between two fingers.

He popped it into his mouth. He chewed. The smile never left his face.

The client stared.

I sighed.

“Mop.”

“What Miss Diddi?”

“Stop eating in front of customers.”

Mop swallowed.

“That wasn’t eating.”

“What was it?”

“Cleaning.”

Quill made a note in the ledger.

“That’s coming out of your pay.”

Mop spat a bit of gristle toward him.

“We don’t get paid rune-slinger! Now shut your ink-hole.”

Quill flicked the offending piece off his ledger without looking at it.

“Someday.” He tapped the page. “Then I’ll have it here.”

Mop narrowed his eyes.

“I’ll chew you next.”

Voop.

Voop.

Both disappeared.

The client stared at the empty stool.

Then at the empty patch of floor.

Then at me.

I sighed. “Grow up, you two.”

Quill’s voice came from nowhere.

“I did nothing.”

“You disappeared.”

“There was a threat.”

“You’re an imp.”

“I am an accountant.”

“Same thing.”

“It is absolutely not the same thing.”

Mop’s invisible voice drifted from the other side of the room.

“Feral.”

“There is no such thing as a feral imp.”

“Feral imp.”

“I object to the taxonomy.”

“Jealous feral imp.”

Silence.

I looked at the client. He had gone very still.

"Never let Hell and Abyss into the same shop. Hell will nitpick you, and Abyss will pick every nit until you bleed. Somewhere in between work gets done, though, so it's not a total loss."

That got half a laugh out of him.

Better.

People tell you more when they’ve laughed.

People tell you much more when they think the joke wasn’t at their expense.

It usually is.

I set the handkerchief on the table.

That's when I noticed Mop had forgotten one of his jobs. He's always forgetting things.

"Mop, the basin?"

"'Mop' this, 'Mop' that..."

The standard wooden basin floated through the air and landed on the table. "You're lucky I'm not on strike."

I smiled at the customer.

"Now price. You're a shop clerk?"

"Peddler. I have a cart, and..."

I cut him off. I hate it when clients try to tell you how hard their life is, and why they can't afford my tells. The way I see it, they can't afford _NOT_ to hear my tells. But I'm reasonable on price. It all depends on... what it depends on.

"What kind of peddling?"

"What?"

"Toys, crossbows, miniature dragons, cookies? What do you peddle?"

"Used items."

"Junk."

"Some say junk."

Mop cut in. "Junk! I love junk! Sharp junk... pointy junk..."

Not helpful.

"Alright, two silver. On the table."

"You said the first taste was free."

"It is honeydew, but I already know you'll want a second question. Now, you want to know about your wife.”

He put two silver on the table.

Quill scratched a note in his ledger. You can always tell Quill's excitement when payment is offered by the pressure of the quill on the page. He sounded especially 'scratchy' today.

“Yes.”

“You want to know if she’s unfaithful.”

His mouth opened.

Then closed.

I leaned toward him.

“Honeydew, if you came all this way carrying her handkerchief and looking like someone buried your dog, it’s either adultery or murder. And if it were murder, you’d have brought something with blood on it.”

From somewhere near the wall, Mop whispered:

“Not always.”

“Mop.”

“Just saying.”

Voop.

He was gone again.

Or perhaps he had never come back.

It becomes difficult to keep track.

The client rubbed his palms against his knees.

“I want to know.”

“No.”

He frowned.

“No?”

“You need to know.”

“That’s what I said.”

“No. It isn’t.”

I picked up the knife.

He watched the blade.

Good.

Everyone watches the blade.

It gives them something to focus on besides the fact that I’m about to do something they’ll pretend later wasn’t nearly as bad as it was.

"Want is... _aspirational_, need is... what we have now.”

He swallowed.

“What’s the difference?”

“Tolerance. You can't tolerate the status quo.”

I looked down at the blade.

"And that's why you're here."

I placed the tip of the knife against my belly.

The client leaned back. That's usually for the best, because the next part stains.

Quill reappeared on his stool.

Mop reappeared behind the client looking over his shoulder. The client was too mesmerized to care.

Mop grinned.

I pushed the blade in.

The first drop hit the basin.

Mop looked down.

Quill looked down.

The client stopped breathing.

I drew the knife sideways.

Warmth spilled over my fingers.

Clients expect me to scream. But have you noticed how much scar tissue I have there, on my belly? I think I've lost feeling from my armpit to my beltline.

Anyway, the client made enough noise for both of us.

“Oh... GODS!”

Then he thought. "Oh... GODS!" That's good when what they think and what they say are the same thing. Means he's not in on it... usually.

I opened myself wider.

The future is not a road.

People love roads.

Roads comfort them.

Roads suggest direction. Choices. Intersections. Places you can stop for lunch.

The future is closer to a fistful of string thrown into a river.

Some strands knot.

Some snap.

Some wash away.

Some wrap around your ankle and pull you under before you ever notice they touched you.

That is why I need a focus.

The object gives me a thread.

The intestines help me follow it.

My intestines.

Always mine.

That part matters.

There are diviners who use cards.

Bones.

Stars.

Smoke.

Tea leaves.

Cowards.

I reached inside.

The client looked away.

“No.”

He looked back.

“Sorry?”

“This is your reading. You participate.”

His face had gone gray. I grabbed his hands. I had to, suddenly, otherwise they all pull away. Well, the repeat customers rarely present their hands to me to begin with.

“You’re watching. And now... you're feeling... HERE. Feel that?”

He watched. He felt. He vomited.

Good. Mop was there to catch it. He likes to show off like that, catching vomit in mid-hurl.

I moved a loop of intestine aside.

Mop leaned closer.

I slapped his hand without looking.

“Mine.”

“I know.”

“You were reaching.”

“I was appreciating.”

“Appreciate from farther away.”

He shuffled back. I could hear the frown on his face.

Quill opened a smaller ledger.

“Consumables.”

Mop glared.

“Don’t you dare.”

Quill dipped his quill.

“One intestinal segment, partially detached.”

“It was loose.”

“Not yet.”

“It looked loose.”

“It was inside her.”

“Until it wasn't!”

I closed my eyes.

Lavender.

Linen.

A woman’s hands.

A silver comb.

A bedroom window.

A man laughing.

Not the husband.

Was the woman, now a man.

Slinking in the shadows.

Drawing a knife.

The husband leaned forward. He wiped his chin. That's good... spittle on the chin can be so trance-breaking.

“Well?”

I looked down again.

The strands tangled.

Adultery wasn't there.

Very relieved. Common things are boring.

There was something else.

New loop of intestine. Starting there.

Something _it_ feared more than being discovered in the shadows of a dark alley.

Wait.

_It?_

Something beneath the obvious secret. Sneaking out of the house at night clearly wasn't the juiciest secret.

That is where the good secrets live.

I reached deeper.

Mop inhaled and broke my concentration.

“Don’t.”

“I didn’t do anything.”

“You breathed.”

“I need air.”

“You’re a quasit.”

“So?”

“So I don’t trust anything you need.”

Quill nodded.

“Sensible.”

Mop pointed at him.

“Feral.”

Quill’s eye twitched.

I found the thread again.

There.

Not a lover.

A meeting.

Three people.

No, four.

The husband spoke.

“Diddi?”

I ignored him.

A wine cellar. Fake identities.

Laughing. A toast. To fooling the humans.

Back to their borrowed lives. Their boring lives.

Laughing inside.

I let go.

Hard.

Too hard.

The vision snapped.

More blood hit the basin, and some spilled over. Didn't expect that.

Mop whistled. “On it!”

Quill’s quill stopped.

The client looked between them.

“What does that mean?”

“Nothing,” I said.

Mop opened his mouth.

I pointed at him.

“Nothing.”

He closed it.

Slowly.

The husband leaned closer.

“My wife?”

I began putting myself back together. This part has to be done quickly... you know, because my skin is already pulling back together at the edges. I once made the mistake of leaving the organs out... well, we don't need to get into that.

“Yes.”

His face collapsed.

“She is?”

“Unfaithful?”

He nodded.

“No.”

He blinked. “No?”

“Not in the way you mean.”

“What does that mean?”

“It means your marriage has bigger problems.”

“What kind?”

I tied off something that probably did not need tying.

Habit.

“First question was free.”

His mouth fell open.

Quill smiled. He enjoys this part.

“I can pay more.”

“Of course you can.”

I wiped my hands on a cloth.

Mop reached for it.

I slapped his hand again.

Mop fetched a jar instead.

Very carefully, he began separating the blood.

Mine went into the clean jar.

Everything else went into the dark bucket.

He hummed while he worked.

The client watched him.

“What’s the other bucket?”

Mop looked up.

“Victim blood.”

The client stared.

I smiled. “Mop, tell the nice man we don’t have any victim blood.”

Mop looked at the client with a nasty smile. “Yesterday, there was this orc...”

Quill closed his eyes.

“Mop.”

“What?”

“Cause mayhem on your own time.”

Mop grinned.

“Fat imp on your stool. You have no life. You envy mine.”

“I am quite content doing clerical work.”

Mop tilted his head. Then he looked at the client.

"Tell the fat imp to shut his ink-hole. Or I go on strike."

He wasn't done pouring the blood into the jar yet. Going on strike in that moment would have been... messy.

Quill hesitated.

“Fine. Your life is _interesting_.”

“Bloooooody blood. Drippity drip. Oooh, another flesh-nugget.”

Mop popped another bit of pink into his mouth and chewed it slowly as he laid the basin down and capped the jar. He placed the jar on the shelf.

Quill murmured as he scratched in his inventory ledger, “One jar of Diddi's troll blood. Check. At least the landlord appreciates me.”

From somewhere far deeper in the shop, a voice rolled through the stone.

“I appreciate silence.”

Mop smiled.

“Jealous.”

Quill threw his quill at him.

Voop.

Voop.

The quill struck the wall.

The client looked up again.

Longer this time.

Then back at me.

I smiled.

“Now.”

I folded my hands on the table, raising them long enough for invisible Mop to run a wet rag under them to get every last little drop of blood. My wound was gone. He stared at that too for moment.

“How badly do you want to know what your wife is _really_ hiding?”

We'll add it to the laundry pile later. Another job I can give Shoes... laundry. I could finally afford laundry then. That felt luxurious.

The next part I really hated, but if you want repeat business, sometimes you have to send your client to somebody else.

"Remember how I said you needed to speak to Greenhalo, two tents down? Well, she _might_ be able to give you the goods on that medallion."

His face gave no expression. He just stood up.

No thanks. He just left.

Quill opened his exit ledger. "Diddi, how should I characterize the end of the session?"

"Success. Failure. Write both."

"Hmmmm. I have no way to record both."

"What?"

"The space is a checkbox. Check for success. Ex for failure."

"Invent a new mark."

Mop reappeared.

"Off-strike. Needed to witness the fat imp getting perplexed." He giggled.

"Feral creature."

"Jealous."

We had one more client that day. It was one of those jobs where Quill's glass barely passed a few grains of sand. Client lied. He burned down his own house. Very boring.

That was it for the day.

Now, you might ask where I sleep. For a troll my age, honestly, you're lucky if I'm awake for your reading honeydew.

I fall asleep right there. Most nights, it's fine. There are lots of night-stalkers in Kaer Maga, but when you help enough people, the night-stalkers are usually on your side.

Which is great for business.

And staying alive.

Night-stalkers have puzzles they have to solve too, you know.

The next day, Fatherdrop the bloatmage... we call him that because you never know when he'll drop by, looked in on the shelf of troll blood. He seemed especially _needy_.

Bloatmages are like regular mages who've figured out that some dweomers are right there in the blood. So they flood their blood vessels. They use leeches too, sucking off the excess before it stains their silk shoes.

The bright side: that extra blood _really_ gives their spells some kick. The down side: overdosing turns them into drunken raging spell-barbarians. Or something like that. Is barbarian still a word, honeydew? I forget.

Quill started in. "Do you have a reservation?"

Fatherdrop ignored him and wallowed over to the jars. His leeches were plugging themselves into a hole that wept blood from his skin. They were barely keeping up.

Mop eyed them anxiously. "Fall off. Just one. Pweeeeez!"

Quill shook his head.

"Sir, I asked if you have an appointment."

Fatherdrop finally acknowledged Quill. But not in the way he liked.

"Your inquisition will decease, infernal minion. You know who I am. I must not delay. The power is calling."

His attention turned back to the jars.

Quill pulled out the Diddi blood ledger, accounts receivable.

"How many do you need?"

Fatherdrop grabbed one of the shelf. Apparently he already had a needle in his neck. Didn't bother to ask. He just hooked it up to the jar.

My blood flowed in. Wow. Didn't know my blood was all that powerful.

His blood vessels screamed... at least I thought I heard screaming.

"BEDSHEETS! THE BEDSHEETS ARE DIRTY!"

Oh no. That's real trouble. He was hallucinating. He knows my blood is powerful, but he can't resist taking a hit of the raw stuff. Didn't refine it. Didn't age it or prepare himself for it.

"FIREBALLS!!!! DIRTY BEDSHEET ... FIREBALLS!!!! MUHAHAHAHA"

The next thing I saw was Mop. The leeches had fallen off, and he was eating them like popcorn, watching the tent like the world was going to end. And he loved it.

So I reached over. I'm usually pretty gentle for a 7000-year-old troll. I worked too hard and cut myself too many times to be ended by an insane bloatmage. No sir.

I grabbed him by the throat. That shut him up quick.

He started waving his hands violently, casting his spells without a voice. I laughed. Shoulda learned metamagic, weirdo.

"You do know that I'm going to have to charge you extra this time? This is the second time this month we've had this conversation."

He looked at me wild-eyed. This may work in the dungeons and out in the Cinderlands maybe, but here in my shop? No way.

"No mainlining blood in the tent. Take it home. Take it to your enemy's house. But no using here. We're a respectable establishment."

Mop was picking his nose. Mop was not counted in that statement.

His excess blood was spewing forth. Well, his leeches had already given up, and so had half his blood vessels. He bled on my box of melons.

That made me... cross.

Don't make me cross.

But I let him bleed. It's the only way to handle one of these bloatmages until they get off their high.

I let him go.

"I cannot fathom why the dirty bedsheets and the lantern being out of oil suddenly made me lose my wits. Your unfiltered sanguinity is most intoxicating... and strange. I had visions of bedsheets and chamber pots. I almost..."

"Look, just no mainlining, got it honeydew? Now pay the human boy to get me a fresh batch of melons or I'll cut open a few new attachment sites for your leeches."

"Of course Miss Diddi."

He left me a purse for the blood, the jar, Mop's cleanup expenses (oh wait, those are free), and the ink that it cost for Quill to document everything... including documenting the free blood cleanup from Mop. I think he attempted to weigh Mop before and after to determine the amount spilled.

In the end, he just logged the whole jar as shrink.

Before he left, Fatherdrop reminded me why I liked him, despite all his nasty habits. "Shall I drop by tomorrow with my brother's latest line of alchemical wrinkle creams? He's worked on a new formula."

Now we're talking. Fatherdrop was such a smoothy sometimes. I almost forgot I nearly _ended him for mainlining my blood and unleashing fireballs_.

"Three jars."

"Of course."

That brightened my mood. I know I'm old, but behind this old saggy troll is a girl that just wants to be loved... by other... trolls... nevermind. I just want to keep the skin folds off my other skin folds. Makes them hard to count.

Mmmm, time for another.

Mmmmm. Melon.

So that's when Rezzen walks back through the tent flap.

Mop was licking up blood, alternating between crying over having missed out on Dirty Bedsheet Fireball armageddon, and ecstatic because, well, he's licking up my blood. Quill is trying to weigh Mop, and Fatherdrop is out the door mumbling about finding more leeches. Mop was screaming about his weight differential between "known states" was off by leeches, more leeches, and infernal crack-tossery... whatever that is.

Rezzen ignored all of it. I envied him. Focus is magic wielded by only the precious few who _can't_ see past the superficial details around them. Just focusing on one.

Dwarves are so single-minded. I wish I was a dwarf. Until I look in the mirror. I hate beards, honeydew. Write that down. Who wants hair in their mouth when they eat? Yuck.

Dwarf meat tastes like hair. I gave up eating dwarf way before I found Kaer Maga.

No, the last meat I ate was special. Too special. But that's not relevant right now. I inherited a lot of things from the last one I ate, and an affinity for melons was right at the top of the list.

### Notes: Madoc, Repton, St Wystan’s, charter, and Cynewulf recruitment

#### St Wystan’s / Repton venue change

* Replace the invented Saint-Martin’s-on-Trent with the real ecclesiastical site at **St Wystan’s, Repton**.
* In 943, the old abbey proper was gone after the Great Army’s occupation, but the church/minster and crypt remained important.
* Cynewulf can spend the winter there under **Father Ecgberht**.
* Ecgberht offers him lodging below the church in/near the crypt; Cynewulf instead chooses to live in the **separate smithy/forge** for three months.
* This preserves the original beat that Cynewulf prefers useful work, heat, iron, and isolation over respectable lodging.
* The forge should likely be a **small detached smithy** within the minster precinct rather than a room inside the main hall.

#### Repton as part of Cynewulf’s healing

Repton gives Cynewulf a much stronger moral setting than the invented monastery:

* **St Wystan’s tomb** lets Ecgberht confront him with the humanity of victims:

  * “St Wystan could have been one of your victims.”
  * The fact that Wystan became a saint does not make his murder morally different from the anonymous people Cynewulf harmed.
* **The ruined abbey / memory of the Great Army** gives Ecgberht a direct comparison:

  * The heathen army came, destroyed, killed, and left wreckage.
  * Cynewulf’s depredations produced the same kind of result.
  * Whatever justifications he had, the dead and ruined have the same thing to show for it: nothing.
* Keep the existing **Galatians / bear one another’s burdens** material.
* Repton gives the negative lesson:

  * *This is what destruction leaves behind.*
* Galatians gives the positive lesson:

  * *Carry rather than destroy.*
* Do **not** rewrite the later Witan scene around Repton.
* At most, add a **single flash-memory** of Repton, Wystan’s tomb, and the shame he felt there.
* The Witan still needs its full flood of lost-year memories and still needs Cynewulf’s decisive choice involving the Viking boy from Leicester.

#### Father Ecgberht

* Current priest name: **Father Ecgberht**.
* Wærstan was considered but rejected because of likely reader confusion with Wystan.

#### Dragon’s Teeth arrival at St Wystan’s

* Madoc and roughly **25 Welshmen** arrive in late February on their way to Leicester.
* At this stage they still travel primarily with **pack ponies**, not wagons.
* They occupy/requisition the churchyard for one night with armed men and animals.
* Father Ecgberht is understandably cautious and calls Madoc inside to establish that they are not ordinary raiders.
* Cynewulf is inside repairing a **door latch** or similar piece of ironwork when the charter is produced.

#### Cynewulf arms himself first

* Rule during his winter stay: while working around the church/minster, Cynewulf’s **armor and weapons stay in the forge**.
* When the Welsh company arrives, he deliberately goes back and puts on his battered warrior kit.
* This should **not** be framed primarily as altruism.
* His first motive is old Blackshield suspicion/risk assessment:

  * twenty-five armed foreigners have appeared;
  * he does not know who they are;
  * he does not know what they intend;
  * he does not know whether their paperwork is genuine.
* He positions himself near Ecgberht partly because if violence starts, that is the obvious place to be.
* Madoc notices that the smith-looking man disappeared and came back looking like an experienced warrior.

#### Madoc’s safe-conduct / military charter

* The border ealdorman negotiates the deal, but because Madoc needs protection across Mercia, the document requires **royal confirmation by Edmund**.
* It should function as:

  * military commission,
  * safe-conduct,
  * protection from being treated as ordinary Welsh raiders,
  * title to lawful booty,
  * fixed-duration authorization.
* Expiry should be tied to a **fixed feast day**, not “X days after the siege,” because information moves too slowly.
* Best current expiry:

  * **Feast of St John the Baptist, 24 June**.
* If the war is still going after that date, tough luck:

  * that is all the raiding Edmund bought for the original bag of silver.
* The charter should explicitly include:

  * permission to wage war against designated Northmen/enemy supporters;
  * permission to take cattle, horses, grain, ale, arms, silver, etc.;
  * right to retain lawful spoil;
  * safe passage through the king’s lands;
  * protection from reeves/officers while keeping the king’s peace;
  * prohibition on harming churches, peaceful households, and those under the king’s peace;
  * **remission/pardon of prior breaches of the king’s peace** by Madoc and his men;
  * a request that bishops/priests provide **holy water for blessing their arms**.
* Royal confirmation should be represented by period-style witness/subscription language rather than a modern signature.
* Madoc later secures a vial of holy water from a bishop using this clause.
* Bishop reaction:

  > “You know we’re going to hell for this, right?”

#### Important flaw Cynewulf can spot in the charter

This is how Cynewulf earns Madoc’s respect before recruitment.

Best flaw:

* The charter says the men are protected **“while they bear this writing.”**
* There is only **one physical charter**.
* The Dragon’s Teeth routinely split into detachments.
* Therefore:

  * Madoc carries the parchment;
  * the other 24 men may have no proof of protection if encountered separately;
  * losing/capturing Madoc or losing the charter creates a major vulnerability.
* Cynewulf notices this immediately because as a Griffon he used to **forge and manipulate exactly this kind of military paperwork**.
* He can say something like:

  > “How many writings did they give you?”
  >
  > “One.”
  >
  > “How many men?”
  >
  > “Twenty-five.”
  >
  > “Then twenty-four of you can be hanged while you’re somewhere else carrying the reason they shouldn’t.”
* Possible second, smaller flaw:

  * clause reserving booty “claimed beforehand for the king’s host” is ambiguous;
  * *before what?*
  * Before the raid? Before seizure? Before sale?
* One major flaw plus one minor flaw is enough; don’t turn the scene into contract review.

#### Why Madoc wants Cynewulf

Cynewulf reveals an unusually valuable bundle very quickly:

* native English speaker,

* Latin,
* experienced warrior,
* understands military paperwork,
* knows how English authority and English soldiers think,
* does not immediately react to Welshmen with reflexive hostility.

This makes Milli’s:

feel earned.

#### Madoc vs Fer-Main / lesson of the Dragon’s Teeth

Cynewulf initially sees the Teeth as another version of the Griffons:

* a band he did not create,
* a leader with wild plans,
* people who actually have a use for him.

The difference Madoc eventually teaches him is critical:

> A warband does not have to become cold-blooded to be effective.

Dragon’s Teeth culture:

* genuine concern for members’ lives;
* mutual respect and dignity;
* shared risk;
* competence without treating people as expendable.

This pays off in 6.5:

* Madoc orders Baldy to run **toward Cynewulf**.
* Cynewulf later erupts because he thinks Madoc gambled Baldy’s life the way Fer-Main gambled men on a gutsy charge.
* Key exchange:

  > “He had a shield.”
  >
  > “His shield was YOU.”
* Meaning:

  * Madoc did not assume Baldy might survive;
  * Madoc specifically trusted **Cynewulf** to protect Baldy.
* Cynewulf thinks:

  > You gambled with Baldy.
* Madoc’s real logic:

  > I trusted you.
* This is the corrective Cynewulf most needs:

  * leadership can be ruthless toward the problem without being ruthless toward your own people.

## Part Two: The Stories We Tell Ourselves

They slept in watches.

Osric took the first watch. He waited until the snores were louder than the crackle of the fire.

Then he unbuckled Barrow's Edge, still in its sheath, and held it on his lap. He traced the handle with his fingers and found the seam of jet.

_Gerðr's Tears_.

His eyes welled up.

"Ealhild," he whispered softly. "Are... you there?"

There was no response.

"I'm coming for you. If... you're still in the snow."

He wrapped his hand around the handle. Every sinew of his hand was tempted to draw the blade.

He pulled it an inch.

The black in the steel drank the firelight. Still, there was no response.

He sighed.

"You wanted me to say the words."

He mouthed the words.

"I was there with you... wasn't I?"

He stared into the seam of jet.

He pushed the blade in.

_Click_. 

Elswyth had woken early. She had chosen the wagon over the campfire, preferring its solitude and the canvas as a shield from the world.

She had been watching Osric through the staves of the wagonside.

"Osric, my shift. Find a soft spot. Get some sleep."

He didn't move.

"Osric."

His mind snapped to the present.

"Sorry... Elswyth. Your watch."

He spread his old cloak over the driest spot he could find and wrapped his new cloak about him.

Elswyth sat up in the wagon.

The world here was not as still as the Charnwood. Life announced itself freely. Horses shifted in the dark. Something small rustled beneath the hedge. An owl called from the trees.

In the Charnwood, the woods had always seemed to hold their breath around her.

She had thought that was simply what forests did.

Now she wondered if the forest had known something she had not.

She waited for Osric to fall asleep. Her hands tapped the rhythm of a song Fussleton taught her long ago, and she found she couldn't stop.

She closed her eyes. Her left hand forced her right hand to stop tapping.

She opened her eyes.

She pulled Monanleoht from its sheath.

The blade caught the moon's glow and reflected it perfectly.

She tested the weight in her hands, passing it back and forth. This time, no one was trying to kill her. No one was forcing her to dance.

She flicked her finger on the edge.

_Razor sharp. Skinning a marten would be easy. Or, wetting a..._

Her hand snapped to her mouth.

_NO... I don't own a cap. I'm no murderer!_

She stared at the handle. The antler fork was an awkward thing, but somehow perfectly balanced the blade.

_Speak the words._

"No!" she found herself saying out loud.

Ketill turned in his sleep, telling the water to back off. "My name is Ketill. And I stay dry."

"No," she whispered.

It didn't respond.

She bit her lip.

_Answers. I need answers._

She tightened her grip on Monanleoht. She whispered the words.

"_ᚹᛖ ᛫ ᚷᚪᛗᛖᚾᛁᚪᚦ ᛫ ᚩᚾ ᛫ ᛚᛖᚩᚦᛖ_" (_We play in song_). 

Fussleton's voice rang in her head.

```
You called by song, and song I hear.
Speak your question. I am near.
```

She whispered.

```
Why does killing cross my mind?
What did the redcap leave behind?
```

Fussleton's voice was as smooth and effortless as she remembered the first night she met him.

```
I am the wood before the red,
Before the cap, before the dead.

That hunger came when I was gone.
Ask the red one what went wrong.
```

_No. The last time, I started arguing with stars._

_Click._

She folded her cloak around the sheathed Monanleoht. She'd rather be cold than tempted to draw it again that night.

She spent the rest of her watch sharpening her new felling axe and wedges to a fine edge.

She woke Cynewulf. He had been sleeping deeply, as though he hadn't slept in an age.

"Wake up old man. Time to make breakfast." She smiled. "Kidding... time for your watch."

"Hmm... I remember being woken by a Frankish boot and answering to Franks calling me _porc_. I prefer your methods."

"Watching a charcoal clamp means not sleeping at all some nights," she whispered. "Sleep is like dying in the most beautiful way possible. And waking is the resurrection."

Cynewulf laughed. "Consider me resurrected."

Cynewulf sat up and brushed his long hair with his hand, matting it down. He rubbed his eyes and stood.

"Get some sleep. I've got it."

He fed the fire more branches.

She waited for a moment.

"Osric talks to his blade."

Cynewulf glanced toward the sleeping Osric.

“Does it answer?”

Elswyth looked down at the bundled cloak beside her.

“Not his.”

Cynewulf's eyes narrowed.

"What really happened to the two of you?"

She climbed back into the wagon and drew the canvas over herself.

"You wouldn't believe me."

"Try me."

She pulled the canvas back to expose her head.

"Try you? I was taught from an early age that simple folk make simple decisions about what they believe. And I found that I'm better off not explaining."

"I'm not simple folk."

"You can still make a simple decision, though. Good night."

Cynewulf decided not to press.

Cynewulf sat for a long moment, staring into the fire.

He remembered the moment he pulled Wodbora from its sheath, and his father spoke to him. He remembered being told he was dead.

_I wonder if he might speak to me... if I pull the blade. I can do that without calling for a witan._

But his hand froze.

_I can't risk it._

He had no compulsion to draw it. He studied it instead.

_Steel wire? Silver? Some form of godly iron? Binding the storm?_

He turned the handle in his hands.

“You'll go blind playing with your sword.”

Cynewulf startled.

Murdock was awake.

"You've been hiding that thing since I found you in the churchyard standing over the Viking's armor."

Cynewulf flinched, laying the sheathed blade down on his cloak. "Not really."

"No? Let's see it!"

Cynewulf's hand snapped to the sheath, locking it to the ground.

"I see. Afraid of something?"

Cynewulf shook his head and stared down to the ground.

“Look, lad, I heard the story of the redcaps, and that brought me straight back to childhood. Don’t be telling me there’s another fairy tale I ought to worry about.”

Cynewulf thought for a long moment.

"Do you ever wonder... about your soul?"

Murdock laughed, loud enough to risk waking up the camp.

"Haha..." He then turned serious. "Why?"

"The powers that bestowed me everything you see... expect me to be a priest again. It wants me to hear confessions."

"No, no it doesn't. Nothing does that."

"Yes."

"No, I'm going back to bed. I'll hear no talk of angels or demons or creatures from the dark. No thank you."

"But..."

"Wake me when it's my turn. Good night."

Cynewulf spent the rest of his watch seeing the fire as punishment to the wood for being wood. He remembered fires he set intentionally as a highwayman. He remembered the smell of seared flesh being branded while he laughed. He closed his eyes to the horror. But the horror took longer to close its eyes to him.

As the others took their turns, they stared into the coals and the night sky. Each heard the stories and considered what they meant. Each found a moment to cherish, and a moment to regret.

Perhaps that's when companies truly form.

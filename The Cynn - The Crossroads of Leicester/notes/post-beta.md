Actual safe conduct:

Let it be known that, by leave and confirmation of Edmund, King of the English, I, [Ealdorman’s name], have taken Madoc of Powys and the men who ride with him into service against the Northmen and those who knowingly aid them.

All former breaches of the King’s peace by Madoc and his company are hereby remitted.

They have leave to make war upon such enemies and to take from them cattle, horses, grain, ale, weapons, silver, and other goods useful to war. What they lawfully win in this service shall be theirs, save what is claimed beforehand for the king’s host.

While this writing remains in force, no king’s reeve, burh-ward, thegn, or other officer shall hinder them, seize their persons or goods, or treat them as common raiders, provided they keep the king’s peace and do no injury to churches, peaceful households, or those who stand beneath the king’s protection.

They shall have safe passage through the king’s lands for the ordering of their lawful spoil and for their return toward Powys.

Let also any bishop, priest, or other lawful minister of the Church who receives this writing furnish them holy water for the blessing of their arms, provided no lawful impediment be found.

This leave, protection, and safe passage shall endure until the Feast of Saint John the Baptist, and no longer.

+ I, Edmund, King, confirm this.
+ I, [Ealdorman], witness and undertake this service.
+ I, [Bishop], witness and approve.
+ [Other witnesses as desired].

## Part One: The Debate

Odin met Freyr high in the summer air, looking down upon the patchwork fields of the Danelaw. It was a month after Elswyth and her family had moved to Leicester.

Mortals were going about their daily lives. They could see every detail: the thin blue smoke of charcoal kilns rising from the eaves of Charnwood, the slow crawl of oxen pulling plows through the heavy Midland clay, and the sharp, square towers of stone churches rising where sacred oak groves had once stood.

Freyr looked every bit the god of harvests and abundance. He had broad, sun-browned shoulders, an ever-present smile, and a tunic woven of gold and silver thread that caught the midday light like ripe wheat. When he spoke, his thick forearms cast wide, sweeping arcs, and his voice was boisterous and resonant, carrying the warmth of a summer afternoon.

Odin was in front of him, wrapped in his dark, wind-torn cloak, his wide-brimmed hat casting a deep shadow over his face. In his hand was his spear, _Gungnir_, gripped tight. He was a cold draft in the middle of August.

Odin began, "I've asked you here for... a favor."

"Haha! I knew it wasn't for my joyous demeanor. But you seem unsure."

"Don't make this any harder than it is! You know I hate..."

Freyr finished Odin's sentence, "... asking for favors. Yes. But you know, of all of _our_ gods, I'm as approachable as the spring rain."

Odin looked to the people below. "I have... a problem. I'm losing sight of the convergences. As you know, there was a time when I could see every convergence in _wyrd_[^wyrd]. I could predict which Viking would betray, which mother would have twins, which wolf would find its way home... and which would not. This new shepherd has quite... unsettled me."

[^wyrd]: wyrd: fate or destiny that unfolds through choices and events toward convergences that become increasingly inevitable over time

Odin pointed his spear at a farmer. "There, that farmer. I had known of a convergence with him and his lost son that would eventually happen. Three winters ago, the boy wandered south after a raid. I knew the father would not find him then. I knew he would return when the apple trees flowered for the third time. It _did_ happen last spring after three years."

Odin closed his eye. "And now? ... Nothing. The boy came back with a cross around his neck."

Freyr studied the farmer and his boy. "You think this _Christ_ is the disruption?"

Odin closed his eye. "I cannot know. The Norns' tapestry is going dark, my Vanir brother, and I don't know why. If I have seen convergences in our people, in our history, then why could I not see this _Christ_ coming? Surely he and his followers were fated to meet ours. And yet... nothing."

Freyr looked confused. "So what of the Norns? Have they nothing to say?"

Odin opened his eye. "Everyone thinks I converse with them. Truthfully... I've never actually met them. No one realizes that if I _actually_ had their ear, we wouldn't be facing Ragnarök."

Freyr took a mocking tone. "And what would you have me do? Do as the Æsir do? Challenge _Christ_ to a duel?"

"DO NOT MOCK ME!"

Freyr laughed. "Ah, there's the Odin I know. It's unbecoming to see you... unsettled."

Odin set his one-eyed stare on Freyr. "You think this is my problem alone, don't you? All our gods, the Æsir, the Vanir, the Nine Realms, _everything_ is threatened. And you see fit to make jokes."

Freyr studied the countryside, and the summer sky. "I'm not concerned, actually."

Odin's eye became a piercing star that flashed with scorn. "_NOT CONCERNED??_"

Freyr spread his arms wide motioning to both horizons. "Have you stopped to consider that life travels in seasons, old friend? Why should this be any different? If _wyrd_ is slowing its reveal to you, maybe the season is changing. This _Christ_ may not be a competitor. He may not be _your enemy_. He may be the next season. And we are merely... fall.

"Winter is coming. I always believed you when you said Ragnarök would end _us_, especially if we were unprepared. I took it to be the winter of _our gods_. I took Valhalla, the valkyries, and the training of dead heroes to be your way of storing grain for the winter.

"And now, I'm just as prepared for this possibility as _Ragnarök_. Because perhaps _Christ_ is truly the winter of _our gods_. If the convergences are fading, perhaps the convergence you know as _Ragnarök_ never actually existed as you thought it did.

"The difference now is, should we fight this _Christ_? Does one fight with winter, or prepare for it?"

Odin stared at the moving clouds as if they might give him some wisdom to answer the question.

"How do I even answer that question, if I don't understand what I've lost? Every god relies on me. ME! I AM THE ALL-FATHER! I am _destined_ to be cursed with the knowledge of our doom. I alone carry that burden. And I alone am destined to lead us through the end of days."

Freyr shook his head. "But perhaps that weight has skewed your thinking. If _Ragnarök_ is not truly a convergence, then your burden is to lead us into our next spring. Not one sustained by human worship, but one sustained by existence itself."

Odin put his forehead against his spear. "But the All-Father is..."

"... is what? What we've all assumed it would be? The _law-giver_ of the gods? The leader through the _final battle_? I think you're mourning your lost title as much as your life. And now, I suspect, you might not have to lose either."

Odin shook his weary head, his forehead still pressed to _Gungnir's_ shaft. "This is all easy for you to say. You gave up your sword to marry Gerðr. You embraced your doom, knowing that your choice would make you die in *Ragnarök*. Now, you see hope. Perhaps you're the fool."

Freyr paused. The warmth left his face. Odin had not seen that look since their war centuries before, a sudden storm crossing a summer sky.

"I _was_ a fool, Odin. A fool to think that I could force Gerðr to love me. I gave my sword to Skírnir. I sent him to convince Gerðr and her father that *I* was the best suitor. They rejected me. Skírnir threatened their lives. Threatened them with curses."

His voice hardened.

"All in _my name_."

For a moment, neither god spoke. Then the anger passed from Freyr's face.

"That was the greatest mistake I've ever made. I came to understand that love without choice is not love at all. So when Skírnir returned, I refused my sword. I wanted the choice to belong entirely to Gerðr. That couldn't happen while I still claimed the sword that had been held to her father's neck.

"In time, she came to know... me. She chose... me."

A faint smile returned.

"I didn't give up my sword for love, Odin. I refused to take it back for merely a small chance at love. And that choice I'd make _every single time_.

"We cannot purchase certainty. Not with an eye, or with a sword, or with a planting of seeds. We can observe cycles. We can learn from our mistakes and hope that is enough. But that is the most certainty we'll ever know."

Freyr swept his arm to the countryside below.

"Look upon my followers. Of course they pray to me for rain, and bountiful harvests, and all of the things that are out of their control. But they are not idle... They plant a seed because they must be hopeful, yes, but they do not plant it randomly. They choose times. They choose specific places. Why?"

Odin's eye followed the farmers below as though he were seeing them for the first time.

"Because they plan. Even beavers plan. This still does not answer why I have lost my sight. So, whether I accept your conclusion still depends on understanding _that_ loss. I do yet see some convergences, and that is why I'm here."

Odin's eye settled on individuals.

"I see an opportunity. I see a convergence of three strangers."

Freyr joined Odin by his side, eager to see what Odin does.

Odin's eye settled on Osric.  "I see a farmer, Osric, he prays to the _Christ_ as well as occasionally to you."

"Yes, his wife is Ealhild. She is one of my most devout."

Odin turned many miles to the south. "And there, I see a charcoal girl, Elswyth, with her family. They pray to the _Christ_ and their _saint_, yet they save coal for your forge-elves."

"Yes, they do enjoy the gift."

Odin turned just up the hill to the bell-pits of Leicester. "And there, making his bells. I see Cynewulf. He is studying to be a priest of the _Christ_, but I can still see the threads of _wyrd_ around him, for now. The convergence suggests he has a fall. He will not be a priest for long."

"I do not know this one. But I see his craft. His father, from the look of it, has craft as well. His capability will see him through his hardship, I suspect."

Odin turned to Freyr. "Why do you say that?"

"Cynewulf works hard at his bell-forge. He seems smart and able. He lacks temperament and experience. Men like him usually discover what they must to survive."

Odin finally turned to the lands just north of York, to the camps of his cult. "There is a fourth, whose convergences I have seen from the day of his birth. And this is where the opportunity lies. His name is Grinjaw. He is a strong believer in Valhalla. By the time the convergence comes, he will be a leader of my wolves."

Freyr's eyes widened a bit.

"So these three will meet a band of your warriors. And you're expecting slaughter."

Odin's eye turned dark. "There _will_ be slaughter."

Freyr folded his arms. His face grew darker once more. "That is a cycle of life. The wolves cull the weak and unprepared. The survivors must learn what they can to prevent it from occurring again. I do not wish it for any mortal, but it is a burden they must carry."

Odin turned back to Freyr. "You misunderstand. I have an opportunity. To intervene. To prepare them. To even the odds."

"What... _exactly_... are you suggesting?"

Odin turned his back on Freyr. "_Wyrd_ is losing its hold, and yet I cannot see how these mortals perceive that loss. And knowing how mortals perceive _wyrd_ now may yet be... "

He turned back towards Freyr. "..._informative_".

He continued. "These three have different relationships to the _Christ_. Osric's faith is occasional and transactional. It is born out of necessity more than true devotion. He would just as quickly abandon it to worship you if he thought that meant more rain. Elswyth's family's faith is driven more by intent, but they still hold to pagan beliefs... your forge-elves to be precise. They would not dare mention it, but I see an obsession there. And Cynewulf, he was born into his faith and lives it... for now.

"Each one of them, if they were to meet my wolves in battle, would almost certainly die."

Odin studied the camps of his cults, looking at each wolf individually.

"What I'm about to suggest pains me more than you can ever imagine."

Freyr rubbed his chin, nodding. "By helping these three mortals, you act against your family."

Odin's eye burned with hatred for his own plight. The words spewed forth faster and more manic than Freyr had ever seen or heard before from him.

"But I must learn all that I can! What I need is a test. Your forge-elves can make three blades, one for each of these mortals. The blades can be crafted to their specific situations. _This is key_. The blades must seem as though destiny itself provided them. They must be delivered in their moment of greatest need."

Freyr paused to consider. "...And then... what?"

Odin's grimace turned to a smile. A dark smile. "How does one react to a gift from destiny? Haha. They will use their gift to save their lives, but that is not the test. The question is: do they embrace that destiny from that point on, or do they reject it? The gift must come at a cost. A painful cost. Much as your choice did, to refuse your own sword and embrace death for merely a _chance_ at having a wife you nearly drove away... Let's see, after the battle, if these mortals make a choice worthy of hope.

"The convergence of Osric suggests he will lose everything. His position in the fyrd. His hearth. Even his family. Ealhild and any children they have... will die."

Freyr cast his eyes on Osric and Ealhild, his face becoming as stone. "Unfortunate."

Odin continued, "His gift should seek to replace what he will lose. But the cost should be to abandon hope for a new hearth, a new family. Embracing the blade is to embrace an eternal summer, Ealhild and his children by his side, a space you will provide in Alfheimr. But to embrace it is to live a death. As he visits his wife, the blade will lay waste to his body. Every time he uses the blade, he shall die, only to be revived when the fight is done. He will have no memory of the battle, only the sweet memory of Ealhild and his children."

Freyr frowned. "An eternal summer? One only appreciates summer if winter comes after."

Odin looked surprised. "And yet you permit eternal summer in Alfheimr, do you not?"

Freyr took a wry smile. "Alfheimr is a home for those who have earned their summers with hard winters. You would give it to a man before he has earned it. The road to Alfheimr begins when life ends. But whether they join the road is a choice. Some choose the eternal summer of Alfheimr. Some choose to stay in their barrows to watch over those who come after them. The season they choose reveals who they were."

Odin laughed. "I'll make Osric choose. And that will reveal who he _is_."

Odin's eye settled upon Elswyth.

"The coaler's apprentice is interesting. The convergence suggests she will lose her family to Grinjaw, and that she will be haunted by one of your lost souls. A redcap, a self-styled _Spotted King_. That creature has repeatedly escaped my _Wild Hunt_, but now I expect we'll know where to find him."

Freyr put his hands on his hips, a stern look grew on his face. "Fussleton. Yes, his fall was most unfortunate. His obsession twisted him beyond recognition."

Odin smiled. "The redcap is not destined to kill her, for she is present for the convergence. I need your elves to guard her. To intercept him should he try to kill her. Your elves will make a blade _from_ him, containing his soul. And _he_ will teach _her_ how to survive the slaughter. To use the blade, Elswyth must make a choice... whether to embrace the redcap's insanity, and invite it into her own mind. To do so is to embrace survival at any cost, to reject it is to embrace the hope that her learned skills are enough, even with the trauma of her near-death at the redcap's hands."

"I do not take intervention lightly, old friend. Even when heroes die, the lesson is for those around them. The god of war might seek vengeance. The god of the _Christ_ might seek to ease the suffering of the living. But the god of the harvest sees a hero's death as eventual, as a lesson for those who remain. One must justify the abandonment of the lesson. But I will consider it."

Odin's eye then settled on Cynewulf.

"Cynewulf's fall will take him far from his _Christ_. His convergence suggests he will be a man picking up the pieces of a broken life. I see the dirt of the Road coming for him, and it will test him. I see the scars that his life will become. And I suspect, the choices that earn those scars."

"I can offer no guidance on this one. Whether he is redeemed is up to him and his _Christ_. I have hope for him, but that is all."

As the gods were debating over him, Cynewulf was testing a new bell. It rang through the churchyard, and Cynewulf stood in contentment. For a long while, neither god spoke.

Odin looked at Cynewulf with a mischievious eye. "Perhaps this blade should ask a different question."

Freyr turned toward him.

"How so?"

"Cynewulf's burden is neither grief nor fear. It is Truth."

Freyr nodded slightly, but said nothing.

Odin continued. "I suspect he will spend years wondering whether his _Christ_ could still receive a man such as him. But Truth belongs to neither of us. We answer to it, whether we wish to or not. I expect the same is true for _Christ_. Not necessarily that it is his master, but that he must respect it."

Freyr's smile returned.

"Now you sound like a man who has spent too much time with farmers, my friend."

Odin ignored the remark, but turned to Freyr. His eye was wide and full of intent.

"Your forge-elves will make one final blade. The _Wodbora_. It shall command my _Wild Hunt_, but no man shall call upon its power lightly. Before the Hunt answers him, he shall first enter a _witan_."

Freyr raised an eyebrow. "A council?"

Odin smiled. "Yes. But not one of men."

At that moment, two ravens crossed silently overhead before disappearing into the summer clouds.

"Thought and Memory shall conduct it. Huginn will pursue every excuse. Muninn will pursue every omission. Together, they will hunt the Truth."

Freyr watched the ravens disappear beyond the horizon.

"You trust them to judge a soul?"

Odin shook his head.

"Not to judge. To uncover."

He looked once more toward Cynewulf.

"The blade shall demand a confession worthy of the power it grants. Not to me. Not to my ravens. They cannot forgive him. They cannot condemn him. They can only refuse to let him lie."

Freyr's expression softened. "And his _Christ_?"

Odin's eye narrowed. "...will hear every word."

The breeze carried the smell of fresh-cut hay between them.

Odin continued, "Cynewulf must confess what he has become. Then he must declare what penance Truth demands of him. If he hides from himself, the ravens will know. If he speaks honestly, the Hunt shall pass over him."

Freyr studied Odin carefully. "And what do you hope to learn from this experiment after they survive their encounter?"

Odin looked towards Valhalla, and the armies training endlessly for the great battle to come. "I need to know if mortals still feel the call of destiny. If they feel that wyrd is still entwined in their lives, steering choices toward greatness like the heroes of old."

Freyr nodded, his eyes full of mischief. "I hope they disappoint you."

Odin laughed, but it was not a jovial one. "I don't care if they disappoint me. I only care if they are fated to disappoint you."

With that, the gods walked back to Valhalla.

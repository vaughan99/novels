# Chapter Eight: Hlǣwhand, The Hearth-Warden

The air in the forge was thick, heavy with the scent of disturbed rust, hot charcoal, and a thousand winters of damp soot. Osric backed against the solid expanse of ancient red Roman tile, his breath coming in shallow, ragged, rattling gasps.

The forge had roared to life when he entered. Fresh charcoal burned fiercely in its throat, flooding the cavern with a heavy, flickering orange light. The anvil, workbenches, supporting piers, and vaulted brickwork stood in stark relief, while monstrous shadows writhed across the walls and ceiling.

His fingers, cracked and raw from digging axles from mud, clutched nothing. The char-stick and training shield were long gone.

Outside, three veteran _Ulfhednar_ gathered around the chute opening, their whispers echoing into the forge chamber. They didn't shout or howl. They moved with the cold, silent efficiency of wolves who had already cornered the deer.

Each landed on the charcoal pile. Each stood to face Osric.

The anvil lay before him, and the fearsome _Hlǣwhand_ spear beckoned.

It felt _known_. _Welcomed_.

Osric grasped _Hlǣwhand_ with both hands and Ealhild's voice whispered in his ear, clear as day. "Say the spell... _mēt sceadu-heorþ_" (_Meet the shadow-hearth_). He knew the words, but he didn't know exactly what they meant. He knew and trusted that voice, more than any other, and that's all that mattered.

"_Mēt sceadu-heorþ!_"

Time froze.

The world went black, but only for a moment. His eyes closed.

What came next was impossible.

He heard the sound of his children playing on the floor, and the soft bubbling of a pot. He smelled Ealhild's summer fruit preserves cooking over the fire. He felt the warmth of the hearth. Before he even opened his eyes, he knew.

He was home.

He heard the all-too-familiar huff of Ealhild in front of him, the sound of a slightly impatient wife whose husband had drifted into his own thoughts again.

"Well, Osric? If I put this to your lips, I might get swatted! Open your eyes. I need you to taste the gooseberry preserves."

He opened his eyes.

Ealhild stood before him, as she had so many times before, demanding he taste one of her latest creations. A wooden spoon held a taste of fruited syrup.

"I used the last of old Thorvald's honey from last summer. When you see him again, tell him to move his bees to the clover on the north hillside. This jar was good, but I _really_ liked the honey from two summers ago." The rhythm of her voice softened his mind every time he heard it.

He blinked.

"Oh, Osric... _þú gamli bjáni_... you look like a draugr. Sit down before you fall down."

She slid the stool across the floor with her foot and gently pressed him onto it. He dropped onto the seat without resistance.

"Have I chicken eggs for eyes, Osric? Stop staring and open!"

"But..."

Ealhild put on her best imitation of Osric. "'_My stomach hurts._' '_I'm full._' '_Let the children taste instead._' There. I've saved you the trouble. If I wanted _their_ opinion, they'd know it."

She tapped his chin.

He opened his mouth. He closed his lips around the wooden spoon, enough to taste the syrup. The sharpness of the gooseberries and honey had been rounded somehow, leaving a faint sweetness he couldn't quite place.

Ealhild smiled expectantly. "Now, what did I add? You have to guess."

He knew he _should_ know. Ealhild had played this game a hundred times before. But his thoughts refused to leave her face.

"Raspberries! I found some when Eaxl and I went for a stroll. Oh, if you find a seed, save it! They're going in the garden."

Osric looked around the room.

In the background, there was a subtle sound.

_Whhhiiiitt... Whhhiiiitt._

He saw Eaxl playing with her wooden toy, a model of Gullinbursti the Boar. She smiled up at him and aimed the little boar in his direction.

"Stand back, troll! I am the King of the Butter! My golden bristles make the butter yellow, and I won't let you sour our cream!"

She laughed, trotting the toy across the floor before sending it flying through the air.

_Whhhiiiitt... Whhhiiiitt._

She then frowned.

"What's the matter, Eaxl." Ealhild bent over Eaxl.

"My bristles... they're gone." Eaxl pointed to Gullinbursti's snout. There were small holes, but no brass wire.

"I will cut your boar new bristles, and that will make him fly again!" Ealhild laughed and tickled Eaxl, and the girl laughed once more.

Osric was confused. "When did she get brass for her toy?"

Ealhild smiled at Osric. "When you were training with the fyrd, my love. I shaved some from an old brooch."

Her older brother, Beorn, was whittling a new button from a piece of soft wood. He looked up and smiled without saying a word.

"Mother, if the holes are too wide, I can carve a whole new Gullinbursti. It would be no trouble."

"That's ok Beorn. It's just missing wire, that's all."

He went back to whittling the button.

_Whhhiiiitt... Whhhiiiitt._

Mathild was cleaning pots. She stared at her father and smiled.

"You'll never guess what I was able to barter for... Mutton! Your favorite! I traded two eggs and a pinch of your raspberry seeds, mother."

Ealhild looked at her elder daughter and smiled. "So that's where the other seeds went! Loki will see you as an equal, Mathild!"

_Whhhiiiitt... Whhhiiiitt._

Finally there was Ingi, sitting in the corner sharpening a hewing spear. He was sitting in a chair, the blade of the spearhead in his lap. He methodically slid the edge of the spear with a hand-held whetstone.

Whhhiiiitt... Whhhiiiitt.

He looked up from the blade and gave a small smile, then turned his attention back to the spear. He traced his fingernail along the edge. He spit on the whetstone.

Whhhiiiitt... Whhhiiiitt.

Osric turned to Ealhild. "When did you let Ingi start sharpening my spear?"

"Oh, _now_ you're worried about your spear. I think you _should_ be worried about harvest. Are the stalks still standing? Have you seen any weeds sprouting? You know how I feel about weeds."

"I... hadn't noticed. Ealhild, listen to me... I wasn't... here... a moment ago."

"Yes, I could tell." Ealhild tapped the side of his head. "You wandered off again. You keep escaping into your thoughts when you should be here talking to me about the barley."

"No, I mean... I was about to die."

"You don't say." Her voice trailed off as she crossed to the door and peered through the crack. "Those weeds again."

She sighed.

"Yes. Three of them, standing right by the hedge."

Osric stood. "I need to leave. You... the children... whatever is happening here... you're all in danger."

Ealhild laughed hysterically.

"I mean it, Ealhild. I... you must be protected."

"FROM WEEDS?" Ealhild could barely contain herself.

Whhhiiiitt... the sharpening stopped.

The children looked up, amused.

Ealhild grabbed Osric by the shoulders. "Are you really so concerned about the weeds? Well then, we have a chore. Sit down Osric, the children can handle this."

"No! You can't send them out there!" Osric protested.

Ealhild put her finger over his lips.

"Ingi, take the children outside and cut the weeds down. While you're there, please stack the wood and remove the twigs. Show your father that weeds don't concern us."

Ealhild turned to Osric. "There, see? Just rest. You've had a busy day."

Ingi rose from the corner, laying the whetstone on the sill.

He ushered the children out the door while staring at Osric.

A smile crept across his face. "I cut the weeds at the hedgerow. The children will stack the wood, pluck the twigs, and keep everything tidy."

He turned to Ealhild. "Isn't that right _mother_?"

Ealhild kissed him on the forehead, moving a strand of his long brown hair over his ear. "Yes, of course. Go on. Let's not keep your father waiting."

It was then Osric noticed... the spear wasn't _his_.

It was _Hlǣwhand_.

She closed the door.

"Now, isn't it time for mutton?"

He didn't remember the candle on the table being lit. He didn't remember the smell of fresh mutton filling the air. But now he saw the flame and a chunk of mutton roasting on a spit in the hearth.

"There, you see? Mathild fetched it while you weren't looking. She knows that's your favorite."

She pinched the edge of the mutton, and it left a touch of grease on her finger. "Here... taste." She put her finger next to Osric's lips. "Let's see if she salted it... perfectly."

Osric tasted it. The taste of the salt-pan dominated the flavor.

"It's fine. A bit salty."

She stared at her finger. "A bit too salty..." she quietly said to herself.

"Let's drive that hunger out of your bones." She fetched the spit off the fire.

He turned toward the table.

A wooden trencher sat waiting for him.

He was almost certain the table had been empty a moment ago.

He reached for the seax at his belt to carve the meat, but it was missing.

"Love, did you forget something?" Ealhild was standing beside him, his seax in her hand. "When you loan Beorn your seax for whittling, you must ask for it back."

Osric had had this conversation with Ealhild a thousand times. Everyone in the family borrowed his seax. And everyone forgot to give it back.

He took it from her hand. He remembered everything about that knife.

But there was something missing. He turned it over in his hands.

One week previous, he had dug a stubborn nail out of the byre's door with this knife. It left a long scratch down the spine.

The scratch was gone.

Osric looked at _everything_. "What is happening?"

The door swung open. It was Ingi and the other children.

Ingi smiled at his father, and laid _Hlǣwhand_ on the resting place usually reserved for his spear above the hearth. "The weeds have been cut down. The wood's been stacked, and Eaxl even removed the twigs. The spear needs sharpening again, _Father_. I'll get to that tomorrow."

Ealhild stepped in behind Ingi and gave the boy a hug from behind, and a kiss on the back of his head. "Isn't he the sweetest boy? The fyrd will be proud of him one day. Now, go help Mathild. The fish need scaling, and you're best with the knife."

Ingi stepped towards Osric, with his hand outstretched. Osric, without a thought, placed the handle of the seax in the palm of Ingi's hand.

Ingi turned to the hearth and slowly walked away.

Osric stared at the ground for a moment, then looked straight into Ealhild's eyes. "Sit down, we need to talk."

Ealhild still stood, but she bent over to put her face opposite Osric's. She smiled once again.

"I think maybe you've had enough for one day."

She slowly leaned in for a kiss.

Then she turned her head.

She blew out the candle.

Darkness.

His world crashed onto the dirt of the forge floor.

His heart lurched, as though struck awake by a smith's hammer. Spring ice-melt coursed through his veins.

He gasped.

His body shook with impossible cold. He clenched his teeth to prevent them from shattering.

He opened his eyes.

Hlǣwhand lay on the ground before him, resting quietly. His last memory of the forge was the weight of it in his hands.

The orange light of the forge still danced as shadows on the forge walls. But his skin was as grey as the dead.

He crawled to the forge wall, and lifted himself up. The wave of heat from the forge was exquisite.

He crawled onto the wall, dangerously close to the fire. He lay shivering, absorbing the warmth.

And then he remembered the Vikings.

His eyes darted around the room.

In the corner, he saw three dead men.

Only.

They had no headgear.

No armor.

No belts.

No boots.

And they were stacked on top of each other.

Their gear had been sorted into tidy piles beside the wall.

Three wolf headdresses.

Three sets of fur armor.

A spear. An axe. A beaten iron sword. Three shields.

Even their coins had been stacked neatly beside three empty purses.

He gasped once again for air. His lungs felt like they too had been plunged into an icy river.

"So..." He swallowed. "you followed me... here?"

He looked around once more.

"Not content... with building... bathhouses... for cattle?"

There was no answer.

"Back to being mad."

He giggled and coughed next to the flame. The _flame_? "Why is there... a fire in an abandoned... forge? Oh right... I'm asking the... fire-breathing... dragon... that... tried to fatten me up. Huh. Huh."

Osric would remember none of what actually happened.

The spirit of the farmer, upon saying the spell, had stepped into an illusion of his old hearth, a glamour held in _Alfheimr_ by Ingrid, the nisse.

Osric's body remained motionless. Until...

Ingi and his fellow children crossed the cottage threshold into the yard. Ingi's spirit transformed. He was not the eldest child of Osric. He was the spirit of Ingvarr the Steady, who helped Ingrid preserve the illusion of Osric's home in Alfheimr. The other children were not Osric's children either. They were the fallen companions of Ingvarr.

Ingvarr traveled from _Hlǣwhand_ into Osric's own body. His shade traveled through Osric's arm and spread forth, occupying Osric's form like a hand into a glove. His companions emerged from the spear's head, breaking into the world as formless absences of light.

Ingvarr, now bearing Osric's body, called himself _The Hedge-Warden_. He stood emotionless as the three Vikings closed in. His eyes were as grey as stone. The body stood firm, as though it had no memory of hours spent wrestling axles in the mud.

His companions called themselves the _Shadow Fyrd_, circling him ominously, ready to help their barrow lord Ingvarr perform _the daily chores of bloody carnage._

Ingvarr gripped _Hlǣwhand_ with both hands. He pointed the tip of its blade at the three and dropped into a readied stance. A slow smile crept across his stony face.

The first wolf-man lunged with a roar, his axe sweeping low toward Ingvarr's knee. Ingvarr did not retreat. The instant the blow committed, one of the _Shadow Fyrd_ slipped soundlessly behind the Viking. The warrior's heel found no earth where he expected it. His footing vanished beneath him, his weight carried him forward, and the axe bit harmlessly into the dirt next to Ingvarr's foot. _Hlǣwhand_ answered with a single downward stroke through the exposed neck. Before the body landed, Ingvarr had already turned away.

The spearman circled left with his shield held high while the other circled right with an iron sword, his shield pulled tight to his chest. Ingvarr's eyes found the shadows, and directed them with just a look.

The spearman thrust. A shadow's unseen fingers caught the haft just long enough to drag its point aside. The spearman's eyes grew wide as he saw Ingvarr begin a powerful two-handed thrust to his abdomen.

He brought his shield down forcefully, parrying _Hlǣwhand_ into a stone embedded in the dirt with a _CHINK!_. He readied his own spear for a thrust to Ingvarr's neck.

He gave Ingvarr a smile. "Ha!"

Ingvarr shook his head.

The spearman's smile turned to anger. "What do you mean, no?"

The spearman felt something against his inner thigh.

Ingvarr looked down.

The spearman looked down.

The spearman had parried the point into the stone, but hadn't accounted for Ingvarr immediately working the pole around the edge of his shield. The blade was resting in the crease between his thigh and groin.

The spearman looked back at Ingvarr, a weak smile on his face.

He tried to retreat, but it was too late.

Ingvarr stepped right and sliced, pulling the edge of the hewing spear and cutting clean into the groin. The spearman yelped and hobbled backward, stunned and bleeding.

Ingvarr stepped back quickly, drawing the swordsman closer.

The swordsman unleashed a flurry of swings, each barely dodged by Ingvarr. One swing put another rip into Osric's threadbare tunic.

Just as the swordsman pressed forward, two of the shadows grabbed his cloak and pulled backward at the shoulders. The swordsman collapsed heavily onto his back, his shield swinging wildly to the side. Ingvarr drove the spear through his chest as calmly as a farmer setting a fence-post.

The spearman hesitated, blood dripping down his leg forming a small patch of red mud. He was waiting for Ingvarr to commit. He felt a hand tug at his cloak, pulling him backward. He stepped back and shook his cloak.

Another hand tugged his shield to the side, opening his ribs. Ingvarr thrust, but the Viking stepped to the side, barely dodging the blow.

A pair of hands found his back foot and held it in place.

The spearman saw shadows pass before his face. Flying left. Brushing his cheek on the right.

His breath quickened. His eyes searched the darkness. Looking for the unnatural force. Listening for its breath. But the air stood still. His eyes settled on Ingvarr, who just stood, staring at him with cold eyes.

Then suddenly, the shadows found his eyes. Where there was light, only darkness. The spearman screamed, as invisible fingers dug into his eye sockets. He dropped his shield and spear to claw at his own face.

The scream lasted only as long as the final thrust from _Hlǣwhand_ into his chest.

Silence returned to the forge.

Ingvarr regarded the three bodies for a long moment, his grey eyes as unreadable as weathered granite. There was still work to be done.

With a nod, the _Shadow Fyrd_ moved at once.

Weapons were lifted from limp hands. Shields were stacked together. Wolf-headdresses were folded beside their owners' fur armor. Knives, belts, boots, purses, and every loose coin were gathered without a word. One by one, the dead were stripped, lifted, and laid atop each other with the same care a family might stack cordwood before winter. Every tool had been sorted, every coin counted, every body accounted for.

The forge was cooling. That, too, required tending. Ingvarr pointed to the charcoal and the bellows.

Two of the shadows lifted fresh charcoal into the fire. Another pressed the bellows to breathe more life into the blaze. The old forge breathed hot yet again, keeping the room warm for Osric's return.

Only then did Ingvarr and his fyrdmen retreat back into _Hlǣwhand_.

The spear fell to the ground, landing with a thud in the dirt. Osric collapsed in a heap.

The household's bloodiest chore had been finished. Ingvarr crossed the threshold of the cottage once more, surrendering Osric's body to await its rightful master.

Once his body had forgotten how cold it had been, Osric fell into a deep sleep.

He awoke to a dark room. The only light came from the soft glow of a dim fire. His chest was frightfully sore. He staggered to his feet.

He loaded the forge with fresh charcoal, and operated the bellows. It was all he could do to see again.

Osric saw the same scene as before. It was twice as horrible as he remembered.

His eyes fell upon the chest by the anvil, still waiting to be opened, and the shield lying beside it.

The shield was round and sturdy, easily better than the one he trained with. The ridge was reinforced with steel, but the weight was unbelievably light. It was covered in leather with a painting of a broken anvil with an oak tree growing through, with crossed hammers below.

Inside the chest was a broad stack of practical garments, folded with almost military precision. A thick quilted gambeson rested over a supple wool under-tunic, while linen wrappings for his forearms and legs lay carefully coiled beside a heavy, full-length stiff waxed travel cloak fastened with a plain iron brooch. A pair of stout iron-shod boots stood to the side along with a linen-lined spangenhelm with a sturdy nose-piece. Beneath the stack lay a shoulder-sling of rawhide with four steel-tipped javelins.

He looked around once more.

"So now the fire-breathing dragon... the king of the straw-daub... the master of cow spas... wants to give me a fighting chance, is it?"

He marveled at the craft of the equipment, emptying the chest and running his hands over every piece. The chest did not remain empty for long. He packed the Vikings' clothing and smaller belongings into the chest as best he could.

He picked up the gift of a spangenhelm in one hand, and an Ulfhednar headdress in the other.

"Well, it's either this or a wolf's head. I rather prefer how I'd look in this."
